| L28 | Phase B0 | **A formatter is a guarantee; a scan is only a backstop — and you need both, in that order.** The B0 pass fixed seven surface-defect classes twice over: once in `lib/format.ts` (templates that can only interpolate through `money()/countNoun()/fmtInt()` cannot emit a violation at any seed) and once as QG-12a–d scans over every emitted surface. The scans then earned their keep immediately by finding what the library pass had missed — because the library only governs templates that CALL it, and the scans read everything: a broken singular inside a distractor RATIONALE ("another boxe still fills"), "1 hundreds" in two old-engine C weeks, "1 kites" in Level A, and — the sharpest one — a defect in prose written EARLIER IN THE SAME PASS by the same author who was building the guard. | Sequence for any content-quality property: (1) put the rule in a library function the templates must call, (2) add a scan over the emitted artefact that proves no template bypassed it, (3) write regression fixtures that prove the scan FIRES and that its false-positive guards do NOT. A scan without step 3 is untested; a library without step 2 silently degrades the moment someone hand-writes a string. And never assume the author of the guard is exempt from it. |
| L29 | Phase B0 | **Ordering matters when a rendering change touches identity-bearing machinery.** Turning on thousands separators looked like a one-line flag, but the corpus's freshness (QG-1), isomorph-disjointness (QG-4), arithmetic audit (QG-5) and anchor audit (QG-11) all key off numeric TOKENS parsed from prose. Flipping the flag first would have fractured every signature in the corpus at once. Landing the four prerequisites in order — input parser already comma-stripping, `surface.ts` matching a grouped literal as ONE normalised token, `validator.numericValue` separator-tolerant, QG-11's embedded-value check separator-tolerant — made the flip a no-op for identity and a pure surface change. The QG-11 tolerance was only discovered as necessary because a money-rendered error-analysis value ("$11,198") stopped matching its canonical form ("11198"). | Before enabling any change to how a VALUE is rendered, enumerate every consumer that parses that rendering for identity or audit purposes and make each one normalise first. Rendering and identity must be separated deliberately: keep `answer.value` and `generator.params` canonical, let the UI and prose format, and make every audit compare on the normalised form. |
| L30 | B0.8 style gate | **A judge that RECOMPUTES finds a class a judge that reads cannot — and it found semantically wrong content that had passed 7,877 deterministic assertions AND a 100% authenticity run.** Instructing the adversarial judges to recompute every distractor and every stated answer by hand surfaced two genuine answer-key errors: D7's Day-5 exemplar taught that 45÷6 "rounds up to 7" (it rounds up to 8; 7 is the drop answer — inverting the exact distinction that week exists to teach), and D8's puzzle key named the wrong missing room of 26×34 while asserting 6×30=80, with a mistakeBank total of 674 where the arithmetic gives 642. Both sat in HAND-AUTHORED prose inside a `manual-review` answer and a mistakeBank entry — precisely the surfaces QG-5 does not audit, because it re-derives *computed* answers from `generator.params`. The same pass also found a whole generator class where a distractor's named misconception would have selected the CORRECT answer. | Two rules. (1) Any LLM-as-judge stage must be told to RECOMPUTE rather than verify-by-reading; "check the arithmetic" and "recompute the arithmetic yourself" are different instructions and only the second finds this class. (2) Wherever a gate's coverage stops (here: prose inside manual-review answers, mistakeBank exemplars, and puzzle keys), write down explicitly what is NOT covered — the uncovered surface is where the next real bug lives. The v2 rebuild made *generated* embedded claims impossible via `verifyFor`; it did not make *authored* exemplar prose safe, and nothing in the pipeline was watching that gap. |
