## Phase 3 search targets (top 5)

### G1 — The internal level map
- **Unknown:** Number of math levels, meaning of level tokens (M1, A-1, 6A, 5B, GM0a/6a/7/8, E/F/G), level↔grade correspondence, per-level scope-and-sequence, weeks per level (Week numbers observed up to ~14). Per E27/E28/E61/E35.
- **Why it matters:** This is the module's spine. Everything downstream — placement, Level→Week addressing, packet generation, advancement — needs a level ladder. Copying theirs is impossible from public data; but any recoverable fragment calibrates ours (e.g., whether levels are grade-aligned or finer-grained, and the ~14-week level length hint).
- **Resolution:** **Targeted further search** — (a) manually view the two blocked interviews: "Live with Dr. Alice" (youtube.com/watch?v=JIyGilhIvjQ) and 1851 "Meet the 'Zor" (flizxvAJ3iM) — franchisee/corporate speakers plausibly name level counts; (b) marketplace sweep: eBay/Mercari/Facebook Marketplace for "Best Brains booklet/workbook/level" listings with cover photos; (c) Wayback Machine on bestbrains.com/math, /bb-connect, and center FAQ pages for older, more explicit level copy; (d) Instagram @bestbrainscorporate + 2–3 large-center Facebook pages for booklet-cover/materials-room photos (flagged in 06:A9). **Fallback if search fails:** original design decision — build our own ladder anchored to the Common-Core-shaped grade guides (E35) with sub-grade levels of ~12–14 weekly units each (E27 hint).

### G2 — Advancement gate mechanics (Pass/Review threshold + reteach procedure)
- **Unknown:** The exact Pass/Review cutoff (observed window ~45–62%, per E31); what "reassigned and retaught" concretely means (same pages redone? fresh parallel pages? in-class reteach time? how many cycles before escalation?); whether a Review week delays the whole ladder or runs in parallel.
- **Why it matters:** The mastery gate is the module's core loop-closure decision. Threshold + remediation policy drive pacing, content generation (parallel forms needed?), and the honesty of any "mastery" claim (E50 shows the gate is where the real product breaks).
- **Resolution:** **Targeted further search first** (cheap piggyback on G1 sources: the Dr. Alice interview and Glassdoor's full 113-review corpus, searching reviews for "review", "retake", "redo", "pass"), then **learning-science default**: mastery-learning criterion at 80–90% (Bloom's Learning-for-Mastery corrective loop — reteach with a *parallel* form, not the same items), which deliberately exceeds their observed ~50–60% window. Document the divergence as intentional.

### G3 — Weekly packet internal anatomy
- **Unknown:** Pages per day, day-labeling, strand ordering within the week (concept pages → practice → Brain Buster → review?), whether any spiral/mixed review exists inside packets, and the 23-page vs 56-page unit contradiction (E34); whether the marketing samples' 3–6 problems/page density holds for interior pages (E64).
- **Why it matters:** The module's daily deliverable IS the packet page. Day-structure, density, and strand ordering determine content authoring templates, session length, and the unlock schedule (E79).
- **Resolution:** **Targeted further search** — (a) watch the 12-min student bbConnect walkthrough end-to-end (youtube.com/watch?v=cvVeL689g9M) and frame-capture every visible page: this alone can settle day-labeling, strand order, and the 23/56 question; (b) re-attempt the app-intelligence sites that 403'd (chrome-stats, apkpure, justuseapp) via different access for additional screenshots; (c) center Facebook/Instagram photo sweep (shared with G1). **Fallback:** original design decision — 6 practice days/week × 3–4 pages, strand-tagged (concept / fluency / word-problem / puzzle), density per E62/E64.

### G4 — In-class session structure and in-center length
- **Unknown:** In-center class length (never published; ~1h implied, E10) and the minute-by-minute anatomy of a session — how one teacher runs 5–7 mixed-level kids (⚠E8): rotation? parallel worksheet work with 1-on-1 touches? how the new concept is introduced per-child at different levels; what "day's assignments completed in class" (E43) covers.
- **Why it matters:** The module's live/synchronous component (or its replacement) must model what the teacher session actually accomplishes — concept introduction + guided first practice. Getting this wrong means digitizing the packet but losing the "a teacher actually teaches" differentiator (E68).
- **Resolution:** **Targeted further search** — the two blocked interviews (shared with G1); Glassdoor/Indeed full review read for session-mechanics phrases ("rotate", "30 minutes", "table", "one hour"); YouTube re-query for any RTP/franchisee online-class clips showing session flow. **Fallback:** learning-science default — explicit-instruction arc (worked example → guided practice → independent start) in a ~10-min 1-on-1 slice within a ~50-min multi-level table, consistent with E43/E44.

### G5 — Retention design under "non-repetitive" (spiral review question)
- **Unknown:** How (whether) Best Brains counters forgetting, given the literal official claim "students don't review old material" (E4/E39). Do packets quietly embed mixed review? Do periodic tests (E32) carry cumulative content? Is Summer Power Up compression (E33) evidence that retention is simply not engineered?
- **Why it matters:** This is the single biggest pedagogical risk in cloning the model: pure forward-marching contradicts spacing/retrieval research. The module must decide whether "non-repetitive" is a marketing frame to preserve (novelty each week) while engineering hidden spaced review — or a real design to copy.
- **Resolution:** **Targeted further search** (rides on G3: packet interiors and test content answer this empirically), then **learning-science default regardless of findings**: spaced retrieval practice + interleaving (Ebbinghaus/Cepeda spacing; Rohrer interleaving) — keep the weekly-new-concept surface, but thread 20–30% cumulative retrieval into daily pages and make periodic tests cumulative. Mark as a deliberate, evidence-based divergence from the literal claim.

## Design-default gaps (convert directly; no further search)

### G6 — Placement test content, format, and scoring
- **Unknown:** Item content, length, adaptivity, scoring, and how the director's "personalized academic plan" maps score→level (E20/E30; confirmed-absent per 06:A7).
- **Why it matters:** Placement is the module's front door and its accuracy bounds everything (wrong level = wrong weekly concept for months).
- **Resolution:** **Learning-science default** — adaptive diagnostic placement: short item-bank walk down/up the module's own level ladder to a stable frustration/mastery boundary (mastery-band placement, ZPD principle), plus a parent-facing results conversation mirroring E20's director meeting. Original design for item authoring.

### G7 — Progress Book format, fields, and comment structure
- **Unknown:** No page/scan of the Progress Book exists anywhere public (E48). Fields, rubric, comment template, sign-off mechanics unknown.
- **Why it matters:** The signed weekly parent artifact is the model's retention engine (most-repeated parent praise, E11/04§3) — the module needs its digital equivalent.
- **Resolution:** **Original design decision** — design our own weekly parent report from the confirmed ingredients: Week code + Passed/Review + % (E80), 1–3 sentence teacher-voice narrative mixing academics and behavior (E48), what-was-taught / needs-reinforcement / improvements structure (E47), plus an explicit parent acknowledge action replicating the signature (E15). (A social-media photo of a Progress Book may surface during the G1/G3 sweep — treat as free calibration, not a dependency.)

### G8 — Teacher feedback-writing guidelines and mistake-diagnosis method
- **Unknown:** How teachers are told to write comments and diagnose errors; training content (E51). Nothing public.
- **Why it matters:** Comment quality is the human differentiator; the module's feedback generation (or teacher-assist) needs a rubric.
- **Resolution:** **Learning-science default** — formative-feedback principles (Hattie & Timperley: feed-up/feed-back/feed-forward; task-level not person-level; one actionable next step) + error-analysis taxonomy per strand. The ANALYZE strand (E59) shows they value error analysis for students — extend the same to teacher feedback.

### G9 — Complete grade-topic map (Grades 4, 6, 8 guides missing)
- **Unknown:** Cary-style curriculum guides were found for Gr 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 only (E35); Gr 4/6/8 not found this sweep (possibly exist).
- **Why it matters:** The grade guides are the best public proxy for scope-and-sequence anchoring (G1 fallback path); holes at 4/6/8 leave gaps exactly at fraction-operations, ratio, and pre-algebra transitions.
- **Resolution:** Cheap **targeted further search** *if* Phase 3 runs anyway (site:bestbrains.com "grade 4 math", /article/MathGrade4|6|8, other center subdomains); otherwise **learning-science default**: fill from Common Core CCSS grade standards directly — the found guides are standards-shaped, so interpolation is low-risk.

### G10 — Timed-quiz / fluency mechanics
- **Unknown:** Frequency, format, and placement of "timed quizzes" (named lever, E38/E54); third-party hint that speed pressure lives mainly in Abacus.
- **Why it matters:** Fluency practice design (and whether to time it) affects anxiety vs automaticity trade-offs for the target ages.
- **Resolution:** **Original design decision**, informed by fluency research: short low-stakes timed retrieval sprints on already-mastered facts only (never on the current week's new concept), self-beating rather than norm-referenced timing.

### G11 — Non-computational strand cadence and proportion
- **Unknown:** What fraction of each weekly packet is non-computational; whether Brain Buster/ANALYZE pages appear weekly, per-unit, or ad hoc (E26/E58/E59).
- **Why it matters:** The dual-strand mix is the visible brand differentiator (E66); the module must pick a ratio and cadence for authoring templates.
- **Resolution:** **Original design decision** — commit to ≥1 non-computational page per weekly packet (puzzle/logic at 6–9, error-analysis/writing at 10+, multi-modal at 3–5), matching the sampled page types; revisit if G3 search recovers real packet interiors.

### G12 — Grading turnaround timing
- **Unknown:** Whether graded packets return same-class or next-week; turnaround between submission and Progress Log entry (04 §8).
- **Why it matters:** Feedback latency is pedagogically expensive; BB's weekly batch is a structural weakness a digital module can beat.
- **Resolution:** **Original design decision** (deliberate divergence): immediate per-page feedback for objective items + human-style narrative weekly — keeping the weekly parent cadence (E11) while collapsing the student feedback delay. Learning-science backing: feedback-timing research favors immediacy for procedural skills.

### G13 — Independent efficacy evidence
- **Unknown:** No independent outcome data exists; only the 2020 self-survey 96%/90% (E19).
- **Why it matters:** The module cannot inherit or cite these claims; and it needs its own outcome instrumentation to avoid the same credibility hole.
- **Resolution:** **Original design decision** — build outcome measurement into the module from day one (placement-to-current delta, mastery-gate pass rates, school-grade self-report at 6 months) and never quote BB's stats as fact.

### G14 — Organic parent demand signal
- **Unknown:** Near-zero authentic parent discourse exists (04 §1/§5); what parents genuinely value vs tolerate is only visible through curated reviews and complaints.
- **Why it matters:** Module value propositions would otherwise be built on supply-side marketing narratives.
- **Resolution:** **Original design decision** — treat the triangulated complaint set (E85–E89: turnover/continuity, wait times, sync fragility, answers-without-explanation, device limits) as the demand signal and design directly against it; the weekly-progress-report praise (04 §3) as the one confirmed delighter to preserve. Further forum search has demonstrated near-zero yield — do not spend Phase 3 budget here (the 403'd Quora thread may be grabbed opportunistically).
