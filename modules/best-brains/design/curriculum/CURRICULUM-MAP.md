## 0. Structural spine (applies to every level)

**0.1 The ladder.** Five levels, A–E (program-owner mandate), each a **fixed ordered sequence of 24 weekly concepts** — our original variant of the evidenced 26-week-per-level structure (per E93) — organized as **2 blocks of 12** with a **Mid-Level Checkpoint** after Week 12 and a **Level-Exit Gate** after Week 24 `[original design]`. The 24-week count aligns with the 4-week test cadence (per DD9: tests after weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 — six per level; the Week-12 test is the extended Mid-Level Checkpoint, the Week-24 test is the gating level-exit test, Pass ≥ 85%, parallel-form retake after one corrective week per DD9 **[DIVERGENCE — evidence leans non-gating tests, per E97]**) and with the 4-packets-per-month materials rhythm (per E99, E34).

**0.2 Placement, not grade.** Level is decoupled from school grade via adaptive placement (per E30 mechanism; DD5 internals `[original design]`). Age ranges below are *typical*, never gating (the model serves students behind or above grade level, per E3). Child- and parent-facing level names are neutral letters A–E, never grade numbers (destigmatization per DD2); the internal level↔grade map for parent reporting is: A≈PreK/K, B≈Gr 1–early 2, C≈Gr 2–3, D≈Gr 4–5, E≈Gr 6–7 `[original design]`, CCSS-anchored per DD10.

**0.3 Age-band presentation law** (per E24 bands 3–5/6–9/10+; E60 visual→symbolic progression):

| Level | Presentation band | Page character (evidenced page-type anchors) |
|---|---|---|
| A | Beginner-style (≈3–6) | Multi-modal, mascot-decorated, draw/color/circle answers, parent-facing Teacher's-Note-style strips (per E57), one-operation-per-page early-years format with draw-the-answer boxes (per E62) |
| B, C | Intermediate-style (≈6–9) | Visual scaffolds — place-value columns with labeled color headers (per E53, E58), balance-scale equation graphics and missing-factor grids (per E58, E37), branded logic-puzzle pages (per E58) |
| D | Transition (≈9–11) | Scaffolds fade across the level; written explanation lines appear; puzzle pages shift toward error analysis (per E60) |
| E | Advanced-style (≈10–12) | Symbolic work, coordinate grids, Always/Sometimes/Never classification, error-analysis pages with "explain why" (per E59); decoration minimal (per E60) |

Algebraic thinking threads through all five levels: icon-as-unknown (A) → balance scale & missing factor (B–C) → variables, inequalities, structured error analysis (D–E) (per E37).

**0.4 Weekly anatomy** (every week, every level, per DD3): Day 1 concept echo + guided practice; Days 2–3 fluency + application (≤2 ungraded 2-min sprints on ≥2-week-old material, per DD11/E54); Day 4 word problems (per E38); Day 5 non-computational page (per DD12) + spaced-retrieval warm-ups woven through all days at 20–30% of items (per DD8 **[DIVERGENCE]**) + weekly mastery check (per DD1, Pass ≥ 85%, corrective loop on miss). Both strands below are per-week mandatory (per E26, E66).

**0.5 Sequencing authority.** Every 24-week sequence below = `[original design]`: my synthesis of the CCSS K–7 trajectory (K.CC/K.OA/K.G/K.MD → 1.NBT/1.OA/1.MD → 2.NBT/2.OA/2.MD/2.G → 3.OA/3.NF/3.MD → 4.NBT/4.NF/4.MD → 5.NBT/5.NF/5.OA/5.MD/5.G → 6.RP/6.NS/6.EE/6.SP/6.G → 7.RP/7.NS/7.EE/7.SP/7.G), scope-filled per DD10, shaped to the six evidenced topic strands (Number Sense & Counting; Addition & Subtraction; Multiplication & Division; Decimals & Fractions; Probability & Statistics; Algebra & Geometry — per E25) as strand *labels* only.

## LEVEL A — Pre-K/K Readiness (ages ~4–6)

### Learning goals
- Count with one-to-one correspondence to 20; rote-count to 50; skip-count by 10s (CCSS K.CC per DD10).
- Read and write numerals 0–20; connect numeral ↔ quantity ↔ word.
- Compare and order sets and numbers to 10 (more/fewer/same; before/after/between).
- Compose and decompose numbers to 10 (number bonds); see teen numbers as ten-plus-ones (place-value seed).
- Add and subtract within 10 with objects, pictures, and number sentences (entry content family per E36: counting with manipulatives, sequencing, number writing/recognition, basic addition to 10).
- Name and compose 2-D shapes and common 3-D solids; use positional language; sort by attribute.
- Compare length, height, weight, capacity with direct comparison and nonstandard units.
- Meet the unknown as a picture: missing-part icon puzzles (algebra seed per E37).

### Expanded weekly examples

**Week 15 — Addition within 10.** *Teacher explanation covers:* addition as joining two groups; count-on as "start big, count up"; the number sentence as a story you can read aloud; why the total is the same whichever part you start from (commutativity, informally). *Guided-example progression (abstract):* (1) persona joins two visible object groups and counts all; (2) same problem re-solved by counting on from the larger group — child taps each count; (3) picture pair with the number sentence scaffolded beneath, child fills the total box; (4) child builds the whole number sentence from a fresh picture. *Daily practice:* Day 1 picture-addition echoing the guided set + warm-up (numeral writing, wk 3–4 retrieval per DD8); Day 2 count-on fluency with ten-frames + retrieval of bonds of 5 (wk 12); Day 3 mixed picture/numeral addition, one optional untimed "beat-your-own" page skipped at this band (DD11 sprints start at Level B `[original design]`); Day 4 join-story word problems read aloud by the app; Day 5 "write a number sentence and solve" page + weekly check (6 objective items) + Teacher's-Note strip for the parent explaining count-on (strip convention per E57).

**Week 5 — More, fewer, same.** *Teacher explanation covers:* comparing by matching one-to-one, not by guessing from appearance; the traps of spread-out vs bunched sets (conservation); the words more/fewer/same as claims you can *prove* by pairing. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona pairs two sets with connecting lines, one unmatched item proves "more"; (2) child draws the pairing lines; (3) near-equal sets (7 vs 8) where counting beats eyeballing; (4) child compares and states the claim in words. *Daily practice:* Day 1 pairing-line comparisons; Day 2 count-then-compare to 10 + counting retrieval (wks 1–2); Day 3 "make it equal" (add or cross out to match); Day 4 compare-story problems ("who has more crackers?"); Day 5 which-has-more visual-reasoning page with deliberately misleading layouts + weekly check.

### Entry expectations / exit mastery profile
**Entry:** speaks number words; can attend ~5–10 min with an adult nearby; no reading required (all directions audio-first at this level `[original design]`); pencil/stylus grip emerging. **Exit (gate per DD1/DD9):** counts and writes to 20; compares and orders to 10; bonds to 10; adds/subtracts within 10 with pictures and number sentences; names flat/solid shapes; compares measurable attributes; reads a missing-part icon puzzle as "find the hidden part."

### Age-band interaction notes
Full beginner-band treatment (per E57, E60, E62): mascot presence high, answers drawable/colorable/circlable, one operation per page in the early weeks, oversized answer boxes, audio directions, parent Teacher's-Note strips on Day-5 pages. Sessions cap at the low end of the dose (≈5 min/day, per E12 range). No timed anything (DD11 sprints begin at Level B `[original design]`).

## LEVEL B — Early Elementary (ages ~6–8)

### Learning goals
- Count, read, write, and order numbers to 120; understand tens-and-ones place value (CCSS 1.NBT per DD10).
- Achieve accurate add/subtract within 20 using strategies (count on, make ten, think-addition), moving toward fluency.
- Understand the equal sign as balance; solve missing-addend problems (algebra thread per E37: icon → balance scale).
- Add and subtract within 100, including regrouping with place-value models (scaffold style per E53).
- Tell time to hour/half/quarter; count money collections; measure with standard units.
- Read and build tally charts, picture graphs, and bar graphs (Probability & Statistics strand label per E25).
- Seed multiplication: skip counting, equal groups, arrays, even/odd.
- Partition shapes into halves and quarters (fraction seed).

### Expanded weekly examples

**Week 2 — Tens and ones.** *Teacher explanation covers:* why we bundle by ten (counting large sets fast); the two-digit numeral as a *code* — position tells value; 47 as 4 tens + 7 ones AND as 47 ones; the labeled-column scaffold (visual convention per E53) as a thinking tool the child will later drop. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona bundles loose cubes into ten-rods, counts "10, 20, 30, 40… 41, 42…"; (2) child drags bundles into labeled tens\|ones columns and reads the number; (3) reverse: given the numeral, build it; (4) trap case — 3 tens 12 ones — rebundle to see 42. *Daily practice:* Day 1 build-and-read with column scaffold + warm-up (count to 120, wk 1); Day 2 decompose/expanded form + count-on retrieval (Level A bonds, per DD8's cross-level reach); Day 3 mixed build/read/write + first 2-minute ungraded sprint (numeral writing — mastered ≥2 wks prior, per DD11); Day 4 place-value word problems ("crayons come in boxes of 10…"); Day 5 who-am-I riddle page + weekly check.

**Week 13 — Addition within 100 with regrouping.** *Teacher explanation covers:* the moment ten ones become one ten (regrouping as *rebundling*, not a trick); connecting the base-ten model to each written step of the column method; why we start with the ones; estimation as a reasonableness check. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona solves with blocks only — physically trades ten ones for a rod; (2) same problem, blocks and written column side-by-side, each move mirrored; (3) written-only with the child narrating the trade; (4) child solves cold, then verifies with an estimate. *Daily practice:* Day 1 model+column pairs echoing guided set + warm-up (make-ten, wk 5); Day 2 column-method fluency, mixed crossing/non-crossing + missing-addend retrieval (wk 7); Day 3 interleaved with-and-without regrouping (interleaving per DD8) + optional sprint (adding tens, wk 10); Day 4 two-step money-flavored word problems; Day 5 spot-the-mistake page (the wrong solution forgets the carried ten) + weekly check.

### Entry expectations / exit mastery profile
**Entry:** Level A exit profile (or placement equivalent per DD5): counts/writes to 20, ±within 10, bonds to 10. Early-reader; directions dual audio+text `[original design]`. **Exit:** place-value fluent to 120; ±within 20 accurate and strategic; ±within 100 with regrouping; equal-sign-as-balance solid; missing addends; time to quarter hour; money collections; graphs; arrays as repeated addition; halves/quarters.

### Age-band interaction notes
Intermediate-band presentation begins (per E58, E60): place-value column scaffolds with labeled color headers (per E53), balance-scale graphics (per E58), weekly logic-puzzle Day-5 pages in the Brain-Buster spirit (branded with OUR OWN puzzle mark, never Best Brains' — legal rule). Mascot presence tapers from Level A. DD11 sprints begin here: ≤2/week, 2 minutes, self-referenced, never on the current week's concept. Dose mid-range (~10 min/day, per E12).

## LEVEL C — Elementary Fluency (ages ~7–9)

### Learning goals
- Read, write, compare, and round numbers to 1,000; add/subtract within 1,000 fluently, including across zeros (CCSS 2.NBT/3.NBT per DD10).
- Understand multiplication as equal groups/arrays and division as sharing/grouping; connect the two as fact families.
- Reach single-digit multiplication and division fact fluency (the level's fluency spine).
- Understand fractions as numbers: unit fractions, the number line, equivalence, comparison (CCSS 3.NF).
- Solve two-step word problems across all four operations.
- Tell time to the minute and solve elapsed-time problems; estimate/measure mass and liquid volume.
- Understand area and perimeter and stop confusing them.
- Classify quadrilaterals by attributes; read scaled graphs.

### Expanded weekly examples

**Week 6 — Meeting multiplication.** *Teacher explanation covers:* multiplication as counting *groups* instead of *things*; the array as the bridge picture (rows tell you the groups, columns tell you the size); why 3×4 and 4×3 land on the same total (turn the array); the × sentence as a compressed repeated-addition sentence — the *why* before any fact drill (concept-first doctrine per E5, anti-drill stance per E67). *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona turns a groups-picture into a repeated-addition sentence, then compresses it to ×; (2) child builds the array for a given × sentence; (3) child writes both the repeated-addition and × sentences for a fresh picture; (4) rotate-the-array moment — child predicts, then checks, that the total survives. *Daily practice:* Day 1 picture↔sentence translation + warm-up (skip counting, Level B wk 18 retrieval per DD8); Day 2 array building/reading + two-step story retrieval (wk 5); Day 3 interleaved groups/arrays/repeated addition + optional sprint (±within 1,000 facts, wks 3–4); Day 4 equal-groups word problems (no fact demands beyond ×2/×5 seeds); Day 5 "which pictures show 3×4?" argument page + weekly check.

**Week 15 — Meeting fractions.** *Teacher explanation covers:* a fraction as a *number*, not a picture of pizza — born from splitting a whole into equal parts; the unit fraction as the building brick (3/4 = three 1/4-bricks); placing fractions on the number line between 0 and 1; why equal parts is non-negotiable. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona folds a strip into equal parts and names one part; (2) child partitions a number line and labels unit fractions; (3) child builds non-unit fractions from unit bricks; (4) trap case — unequal partition offered as "fourths," child rejects and repairs it. *Daily practice:* Day 1 partition-and-name with strips and lines + warm-up (÷ facts, wks 9–10); Day 2 number-line placement fluency + area retrieval seed... (area arrives wk 20; retrieval draws backward only — substitute ×8/×9 facts, wk 12) `[authoring note: retrieval pools always draw from earlier weeks only]`; Day 3 unit-brick composition, interleaved with equal/unequal-part judgments; Day 4 fraction word problems (sharing contexts); Day 5 equal-parts sort with adversarial partitions + weekly check.

### Entry expectations / exit mastery profile
**Entry:** Level B exit profile: ±within 100 with regrouping, arrays as repeated addition, halves/quarters. **Exit:** ±within 1,000 fluent; ALL single-digit ×/÷ facts accurate (rate self-referenced only, per DD11); fractions as numbers with equivalence/comparison; two-step problems; time/mass/volume; area vs perimeter mastered; quadrilateral classification; scaled graphs.

### Age-band interaction notes
Full intermediate-band treatment (per E58, E60): missing-factor grids, balance-scale equations, weekly logic pages. Fact fluency is built by strategy-then-retrieval (per DD8/DD11), never by timed drill sheets — the sharpest anti-Kumon commitment of the level (per E67, E70; ungraded-timing philosophy per E54). Written "explain" lines begin appearing on Day-5 pages in Block 2 (transition prep for Level D, per E60 progression).

## LEVEL D — Upper Elementary (ages ~9–11)

### Learning goals
- Read/write/compare/round whole numbers to 1,000,000; fluent multi-digit addition/subtraction (CCSS 4.NBT per DD10).
- Master multi-digit multiplication (area model → standard algorithm) and division with 1- and 2-digit divisors, interpreting remainders.
- Understand factors, multiples, primes; multiplicative comparison.
- Operate on fractions: equivalence, ±(like → unlike denominators), ×fractions, ÷ with unit fractions (CCSS 4.NF/5.NF).
- Understand decimals to thousandths; ± × ÷ decimals; connect fractions ↔ decimals.
- Use order of operations; read/write/evaluate numerical expressions (5.OA); plot on the first-quadrant coordinate plane.
- Measure/classify angles; classify triangles and the quadrilateral hierarchy; compute volume.
- Sustain written mathematical explanation as a normal part of work (per E60 band shift).

### Expanded weekly examples

**Week 17 — Adding & subtracting fractions with unlike denominators.** *Teacher explanation covers:* why unlike pieces can't be counted together (the unit-brick argument from Level C wk 15, resurfaced); renaming as *re-cutting the same amounts into same-size pieces* — the quantity never changes, only its name; choosing a common denominator (any common multiple works; the least one is just tidy); benchmark estimation first so wrong answers *feel* wrong. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona tries to add unlike pieces directly, hits the "what do I even call this?" wall — motivating the rename; (2) both fractions re-cut on parallel number lines, child watches the amounts hold still; (3) child performs the rename with the scaffold showing the scaling factors; (4) child solves cold, estimate-first (">1 or <1?"); (5) mixed-number case with regrouping across the whole. *Daily practice:* Day 1 rename-and-add with number-line scaffold + warm-up (equivalence, wk 9 retrieval); Day 2 ± fluency, unlike denominators, scaffold fading + decimal-compare retrieval (wk 13); Day 3 interleaved like/unlike/mixed-number items (interleaving per DD8 — blocking would hide the discrimination) + optional sprint (× facts, Level C wk 12); Day 4 multi-step fraction word problems (recipe, distance, leftover contexts); Day 5 "refute the tops-and-bottoms shortcut with a picture" written page + weekly check.

**Week 6 — Division with remainders.** *Teacher explanation covers:* division as repeated sharing until you can't; the remainder as "what's left when fair sharing stops"; the written method as bookkeeping for the sharing story (each digit-step = sharing at one place value); the multiplication check. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona shares base-ten blocks unevenly, remainder appears physically; (2) same computation with blocks and the written method side-by-side; (3) child runs the written method narrating "share, multiply, subtract, bring down"; (4) child solves cold and verifies via quotient×divisor+remainder. *Daily practice:* Day 1 model+method pairs + warm-up (factor pairs, wk 3); Day 2 written-method fluency, remainder/no-remainder interleaved + multiplicative-comparison retrieval (wk 4); Day 3 mixed with area-model multiplication (wk 5) to force operation discrimination; Day 4 division stories where the remainder must be *used* (buses, ribbons, teams); Day 5 four-interpretations page (round up / drop / remainder-as-answer / share the remainder) + weekly check.

### Entry expectations / exit mastery profile

## LEVEL E — Middle-School Readiness (ages ~10–12)

### Learning goals
- Reason with ratios, rates, and unit rates; recognize and use proportional relationships and the constant of proportionality (CCSS 6.RP/7.RP per DD10).
- Command percent: concept, computation, and applications (tax, tip, discount, interest).
- Extend the number system: negative numbers, absolute value, all four operations on rational numbers (6.NS/7.NS).
- Divide fractions fluently, completing fraction arithmetic.
- Read, write, evaluate, and transform algebraic expressions; solve one- and two-step equations and inequalities (6.EE/7.EE) — the capstone of the E37 algebra thread.
- Work the four-quadrant coordinate plane.
- Compute area/circumference of circles, surface area and volume of solids.
- Summarize data with measures of center/variability and interpret distributions; quantify chance with simple and compound probability (6.SP/7.SP; Probability & Statistics strand label per E25).

### Expanded weekly examples

**Week 13 — One-step equations.** *Teacher explanation covers:* the equation as a balanced scale (the same balance the child met at Level B wk 6 — per E37's thread, now with x); solving as *undoing* — one inverse operation returns x alone; why you must do the same thing to both sides (the balance argument, not a rule); checking by substitution as self-verification, not teacher-pleasing. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona solves x+7=12 on a drawn scale, removing 7 from both pans; (2) same equation symbolically, each line mirrored to a scale move; (3) child solves ax=b deciding *which* inverse undoes; (4) child solves cold across all three forms and substitutes back; (5) trap case — subtract-from form (9−x=4) flagged as "next week's tool works better," honesty about scope `[original design]`. *Daily practice:* Day 1 scale-mirrored solving + warm-up (like terms, wk 11 retrieval); Day 2 inverse-operation fluency across forms + integer ± retrieval (wk 8); Day 3 interleaved one-step forms plus expression-evaluation distractors (must *notice* what is and isn't an equation, interleaving per DD8) + optional sprint (rational-number ops, wk 9); Day 4 equations-from-stories (write, then solve); Day 5 balance-proof page (justify each move keeps balance) + weekly check.

**Week 15 — Inequalities.** *Teacher explanation covers:* inequality as a *set* of answers, not one answer — the deep shift from equations; number-line graphing with open/closed endpoints as the picture of that set; testing candidate values as the universal verification tool; why the solution set is infinite yet precisely described. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona tests several values against x+3>7, discovers the boundary; (2) child graphs the discovered set, choosing the endpoint style; (3) solve-then-graph on a fresh one-step inequality; (4) reverse: given a graph, write the inequality; (5) classification moment — is a given value always/never/sometimes in the set? *Daily practice:* Day 1 test-and-graph echoing guided set + warm-up (two-step equations, wk 14); Day 2 solve/graph fluency both directions + proportionality retrieval seed (wk 16 not yet taught — substitute percent-of, wk 3) `[authoring note: backward-only retrieval]`; Day 3 interleaved equations-vs-inequalities (the discrimination is the point); Day 4 constraint word problems ("at least," "no more than"); Day 5 Always/Never classification for an inequality with conceptual short answer and number-line graph (page format per E59) + weekly check.

**Week 1 — Ratios.** *Teacher explanation covers:* ratio as a *relationship between* quantities, not a quantity (3:2 says how the numbers travel together); equivalent ratios as the same relationship at a different size; the ratio table as a structure-preserving machine; part:part vs part:whole distinction early, because it is the classic misconception. *Guided-example progression:* (1) persona scales a 2:3 paint recipe up and down in a table, relationship audibly constant; (2) child fills missing table cells both multiplicatively and additively-wrong-then-corrected (the classic error, surfaced deliberately); (3) child builds a table from a stated ratio; (4) part:part vs part:whole sort on one scenario. *Daily practice:* Day 1 table-filling + warm-up (fraction ÷, Level D wk 19 retrieval — cross-level per DD8); Day 2 equivalent-ratio fluency + decimal ×÷ retrieval (Level D wk 20); Day 3 interleaved part:part/part:whole items; Day 4 recipe/speed/mixture ratio stories; Day 5 which-is-more-lemony justification page + weekly check.
