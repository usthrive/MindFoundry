MindFoundry · new module preview · Grade 2, beside his class's Chapter 3

How subtraction questions would look

Six question types from the new module's subtraction unit, the way they would appear on his tablet. Each one is live: try it with him if you like. Nothing is saved, and the numbers are original, never his school test's.

If we commit today

He sees nothing new. A commit only saves the curriculum documents. This module has no app code yet, so even a push and merge would redeploy the same app he uses now.

He answers on the screen

Every answer and every marked step goes into the app: digits into column boxes, the regroup marks, number-line jumps, a number bond, a bar. That is how he shows his work here, and the app checks each step.

No photos of paper

The app never asks for a photo. It could not mark one reliably, and a photo would send his handwriting, and possibly his name, to an outside service. Once a unit he draws one model on paper; the app records "paper practice done" for you to glance at.

What counts as an answer

He does
Picks jumps (−20, then −8, or any route) and types each landing before the next jump is drawn. The line never computes a landing for him.
The app checks
Every landing, then the final answer, 46. His route is his choice; the strategy question has no wrong answer.

What counts as an answer

He does
Taps the 6 to cross it out and writes 5 above; writes 13 above the 3; types the answer digits ones → tens → hundreds, on the pad or a keyboard.
The app checks
Each answer digit against the computed column (334). The regroup marks are his written working: recorded for you to see, like the marks on paper, not scored on their own.
If he writes 346
"Smaller from larger" in the ones (9 − 3). The first hint asks whether 3 ones are enough to take 9 away. Each Check climbs one rung; no rung states the answer.
On the school test
These are the same marks he writes on paper under "show your work".
Working backwards · a hidden part

The number bond with an empty part

Question 4 of 6 today
T
The teacher

The whole is 530. One part is 284. What is the other part?

What counts as an answer

He does
Types the missing part, 246, into the empty circle. He may work it any way he likes.
The app checks
The number, computed as 530 − 284. After a right answer the teacher links it: "this is your Kumon subtraction, used backwards."
If he writes 354
That is each column alone, smaller from larger. His test's missing-addend miss came from the same rule.
Why it is here
Missing numbers were two of his four misses. A part-whole picture makes "use subtraction" visible.

Find the mistake · school test format

Fix it first, then say why

Question 6 of 6 today
T
The teacher

Sam worked out 702 − 146 like this.

702 −146 644
Step 1 · Fix it: what is 702 − 146?
Step 2 · True or false?
In the ones, Sam did 6 − 2.
Sam opened a hundred before he subtracted.
Sam's answer, 644, is too big.
Step 3 · Tell the teacher why

What counts as an answer

The order matters
He fixes the sum first (556), before any statement about the mistake is shown, so the statements cannot hand him the fix.
The app checks
The fixed answer, and each True/False row. These are the gradable parts of "what is the mistake?"
The "why"
His sentence is read kindly and answered, but never scored. Spelling never counts. His test lost a point here: he found where the digits go but not why.
On the school test
The same task, written on paper. The app trains the order: fix, check the claims, say why.

What this preview is not

It is not the app. The real module adds the teacher's voice, the lesson before each question type, the blocks on a place-value chart, spaced returns, and the record that tells you what he knows. Help here climbs the same ladder the app uses: where to look, then a fact he knows, then a way to finish, never the answer at the first steps.
