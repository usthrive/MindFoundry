## 2. Item types

| Kind (skill) | Screen | Graded |
|---|---|---|
| bond | a number bond, the asked circle dashed with a "?" (N-I2, N-F1) | the part/whole tap; the typed number |
| box | the sentence alone; after a right tap, the bond with the empty circle where he said | the tap; the typed number |
| read | a supplied bar (N-F4) with the ? on the line over the bar or on a piece | which is the whole (3); the sentence (4) |
| match | a story; three bars drawn **equal** (the decision is where the ? and numbers sit; N-F4 note) | the bar tapped |
| labelWhole / labelPart | the bar drawn, every place an empty slot; chips: the two numbers and "?" (N-I5 step 3) | the labels (whole slot exact; the two pieces in either order) |
| decide | "Does the question ask for the whole or a part?" (no bar, no ? yet), then the label | both |
| build | the label with a **self-check**: on a miss only the places that differ turn orange (the story's bar is not shown); when right, his bar is shown beside the story's bar. The **shape** (one bar in two parts / two bars / one bar in three parts, drawn) is a warm-up once a session, never counted, because its answer never changes | labels |
| solve | the story and an answer box; any sound method earns it (DD9) | the answer; the optional bar never counts |
| paper | "Draw the bar on paper. Put the numbers and the ? on it." → "I drew it" | **not graded** (DD8); the answer after it is graded |

Bar drawing (inline SVG): one rectangle split into two pieces proportional to their values, each at
least 18 % of the bar (clamped); a brace over the bar carrying the whole or "?", said to him as "the
line over the whole bar" (DD20); the child word for a part of the bar is "piece". Label bars are drawn
in their pre-answer state: every slot empty. Slots work by tap (slot then chip, or chip then slot)
and by keyboard (Tab to a slot, Enter, arrows on the chips, Enter places; focus moves to the next
empty slot).

## 3. Numbers

| Range | Whole | Parts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| within 20 | 11–20 | 2 or more each | sessions 3 and 4 only |
| within 100 | 30–99 | 10 or more each, no round tens | trades as the session asks |
| three-digit | 230–999, not a round hundred | 101 or more each, never a round hundred; not both round tens | sessions 2–6: at most one trade; session 8: every item a trade, 2 with two |

- The two parts are never equal. **Keys never repeat within a session**, and a twin's or
  look-again's key is never a key met that day (so it is never the answer just shown at rung 4).
- Penguin stories always have fewer babies than grown-ups.
- **No-exam-items gate (EXAM-COVERAGE §4).** No operand, key, choice or shown wrong value is a value
  on the exam list (tiers 1 and 2, everywhere on this page). The exam's values are **not** in the
  page: it carries a 256-bit hash mask made by `bar_models_check.js --emit-mask`, which reads the exam
  list at run time. About four ordinary numbers share each bucket, so a set bit names no value; the
  check proves every listed value is covered and prints counts only.
- Worked-example numbers of the G2-U03 / G2-U05 / G2-U05-CK / G2-U06 L1 rows are never an operand of
  the check (21 and up; CURRICULUM-MAP §7.12) and never a three-digit operand anywhere.
- The two parts are drawn independently (every part pair equally likely).
- **Fresh forms (W5).** No operand tuple repeats within a session (twins and look-again included).
  Form c of session s draws only from hash lane (s + c) mod 2, so a re-sit never repeats the form
  before it and two sessions in a row never share a tuple (so sessions 3 and 4 of one form never share
  a within-20 pair). An extra-practice form excludes every tuple of the forms he has sat in 1–10.
- **Within 20, honestly:** only 28 pairs with wholes 11–20 pass the exam mask (13–15 a lane), so
  within-20 pairs do recur across forms (re-sits, later rounds). When a twin or look-again finds no
  within-20 pair with an unused key, it steps up to within 100; this is recorded on the item and
  counted by the checker (21 of 494,000 items in the 2,000-form run, all twins or look-agains).
- A review value he could not type (more digits than the box takes) is never used (LEARNINGS S7).

## 5. Misconception rules (every wrong choice and review value is computed in code)

Values (p: whole W, known part k, key = the ?; for the whole asked, parts a and b):

| Rule | When | Value |
|---|---|---|
| addWhenPart — added the two numbers when a part was asked | part | W + k |
| sfl — forgot the trade (smaller digit from larger in each place) | part, a trade | Σ \|W_d − k_d\| · place |
| noReduce — traded, but did not take one from the next place | part, a trade | Σ (W_d < k_d ? W_d + 10 − k_d : W_d − k_d) · place |
| countStart — counted the start number as a jump | part | key + 1 |
| oneShort — stopped one jump short | part (choices) | key − 1 |
| tenOff — took one ten too many | part | key − 10 |
| hundredOff — took one hundred too many | part, 3-digit (choices) | key − 100 |
| extraTen — counted one ten too many | both | key + 10 |
| takeWhenWhole — took one part from the other when the whole was asked | whole | \|a − b\| |
| noCarry — forgot the carry | whole, a carry | Σ ((a_d + b_d) mod 10) · place |
| carryWhole — wrote a place total of ten or more whole | whole, a carry | the place totals written side by side |
| countStartOn — counted the start number when counting on | whole | key − 1 |

A value is shown only if it is above 0, differs from the key and from every earlier value, and is
not masked; **collisions are resolved by the priority order** of the list the step uses (answer
boxes, part: addWhenPart, sfl, noReduce, countStart, tenOff; whole: takeWhenWhole, noCarry,
carryWhole, countStartOn, extraTen; `a + b = □` drops takeWhenWhole). Choice pools (A1): addWhenPart,
sfl, noReduce, countStart, extraTen, tenOff, hundredOff, oneShort; the draw needs three reachable
ranks.

Text choices (every wrong one is false for the bar; the check evaluates each sentence with ? = key):

| Choice | Wrong option → rule |
|---|---|
| part or whole? | "The whole" for a part → boxAsWhole / qAsWhole; "A part" for the whole → boxAsPart / qAsPart |
| which is the whole? | a part number → partAsWhole; the ? when it is a part → qAsWhole |
| sentence (part asked; key `W − k = ?` or `k + ? = W`) | `W + k = ?` addSeen · `? − k = W` qAsWhole · `W + ? = k` wholeAsPart |
| sentence (whole asked; key `a + b = ?`) | `big − small = ?` takeSeen · `small + ? = big` bigPartWholeAdd · `big − ? = small` bigPartWholeTake |
| story → bar (part asked) | ? on the line over the whole bar → qOnBrace; the whole on a part → wholeAsPart |
| story → bar (whole asked) | ? on a part, the bigger part over the bar → qOnPartBig; the smaller → qOnPartSmall |
| shape (warm-up) | two bars → twoBars ("Two bars are for comparing. Here everything goes in one group."); one bar in three parts → threeParts |
| label (graded, not a choice) | ? on a part when the whole is asked → qOnPart; ? over the bar when a part is asked → qOnBrace; a part number over the bar → partOnBrace |

**Choice order.** "The whole · A part" is always in that order (a fixed two-way, so its layout never
moves); its key is balanced by the session's mix (≤ 60 % either way over draws). Every 3- and
4-choice pick rotates the key's displayed place (≤ 40 % on any place over draws); numeric picks
also rotate the key's rank (≤ 40 % on any rank).

## 6. Ladders (4 rungs; Help, or a wrong Check, climbs one)

Rungs 1–3 contain **no numbers**; rung 1 is an orienting question; rung 4 is the answer (with the
model filled). No rung below 4 can state the answer. A wrong Check gives the rule's clue (if his
answer matches a rule) plus the next rung, up to rung 3; rung 4 only on Help after rung 3 or on a
fourth different wrong answer.

| Step | 1 (orient) | 2 (use what you know) | 3 (a way to finish) |
|---|---|---|---|
| decide, bond | Which circle is on top? | The top circle is the whole. The others are parts. | Find the ?. Is it in the top circle? |
| decide, sentence with + | Which number is made of the other two? | The whole is what the two parts make. | The two numbers being added are the parts. |
| decide, sentence with − | Which number is made of the other two? | The whole is what the two parts make. | You start with the whole, then take a part away. |
| decide, story (no bar, no ? on screen) | What does the question ask for? | The whole is everything before some go. Or everything after some come. | Is the whole in the story? Or must we find it? |
| find, part | Which number is the whole? | A part is the whole take away the other part. | Count up from the part you know to the whole. |
| find, whole | Which numbers are the parts? | The whole is the two parts put together. | Add the ones first. Then the tens (then the hundreds). |
| label | What does the question ask for? | The line over the whole bar is the whole. The pieces of the bar are the parts. | The ? goes where the number we must find belongs. |
| sentence | Look at your bar. Where is the ?? | (part) A part is the whole take away the other part. / (whole) Parts put together make the whole. | Find the sentence that does that. |
| read: which is the whole | What does the line over the whole bar show? | It goes over both parts. That is the whole. | Tap what is on that line. |
| match | What does the question ask for? | The whole goes on the line over the whole bar. | Find the bar with the ? in that place. |
| shape | Is the story about one set or two? | One set split into two parts is one bar. | Find one bar cut into two parts. |
| add-back | What should the two parts make? | Add your answer and the part you know. | Add the ones first. |

## 7. Wrong-work practices (BUILD-PLAN §13)

- **W1** after rung 4 (the answer shown) on a session question, a same-shape **twin** with fresh
  numbers follows at once ("Now one like it, with new numbers"); recorded as review, never counted.
  Its key is never a key met that day.
- **W2** after the last question: up to **2** of the day's first-try misses, one at a time, opening
  on the step he missed (earlier steps shown done), **his own answer shown** ("You wrote 37." /
  "You chose …" / "You put …"), rung 1 shown, one more go; then the answer. Score unchanged. Skipped
  if he takes the rest.
- **W5** a look-again for a question whose answer was shown (rung 4) is a fresh twin, never the
  shown numbers; re-sits and extra practice are fresh forms (§3).
- **W6** the same answer again gets the clue again ("You wrote X again." / "You chose …" / "That is
  the same bar as before."); the third time "… Let's read it together." and the question is read
  aloud. A repeat is not a new try.
- **W4** the check's review (§4).

## 8. Teaching rules (binding)

- The decision is always made from the bar or the bond ("is the ? the whole or a part?"), never
  from words. No "altogether", no "in all", no "… means add/subtract" anywhere.
- **"left" and "more" on both sides** (second teacher read). Each story frame is tagged with the word
  it carries; the bank has "left" in whole-asked stories ("… are left. How many at first?") and
  part-asked ones ("How many are left?"), and "more" likewise ("… more got on. How many now?" /
  "How many more came in?"). Each form plans its words: a word used in a form is used on a
  whole-asked AND a part-asked story; with two or more stories on each side, both words are used on
  both sides; sessions 4 and 5 end with one question of the other kind, so one of the two words is on
  both sides there and the other is not used. "at first" also sits on both sides.
- **The setting never gives the decision away.** The bank is written per setting (16 settings, 72
  frames); every setting has whole-asked and part-asked stories, and the share of whole-asked stories
  per setting over all sessions stays between 15 % and 85 % (checked).
- **Read-aloud names positions only.** Before his tap, no spoken line ties "whole" or "part" to a
  number or to the ?: "The top circle has a question mark. The circles below show 45 and 38." / "The
  line over the bar has a question mark. The bar shows 3 and 8." / "A box take away 74 is 13."
- **Help names only what is on the screen.** A line may say "?", "bar", "box" or "circle" only when
  that thing is on the screen at that step (the paper step, which asks him to draw one, is the only
  exception). So the story decision asks "Does the question ask for the whole or a part?", and a
  solve item's clue says "You are finding a part. A part is smaller than the whole."
- Words: "step" for the screen's steps (Next step, Step 1 of 2, every step right); "part" only in
  the maths; "box" only for the unknown in a sentence (crayons come in a pack; eggs in a carton);
  "points", never "marks"; prompts in sentence case. Three-digit clues: "Check the ones again." /
  "Ten ones make one ten. Put it with the tens." (no counting-by-ones clues).
- The grown-up line under each question ("first try … help used …") sits behind a small "For the
  grown-up" toggle, closed by default; "help used" is never on the child's screen.
- Stories: two sentences + one question, at most 10 words a sentence (every child-facing sentence
  on the page is held to 10). No person's names (roles only: a baker, a ferry, a library), so no
  name can collide with a child's. Settings never from G1-U02 (no ducks/pond, birds/fence,
  apples/bowl, boats/dock, shells/bucket, fish/tank, stickers/page, balloons/party).
- Every answer box asks for one quantity (S6): "[ ] rolls", "? = [ ]", "45 + [ ] = 82".
- Help lines point only at what is on the screen (the bar, the line over it, the circles, the
  numbers you see). No "wrong", no %, no speed, no clock for the child.
- Read aloud on tap ("Read to me"), as on the test-prep page; "−" is said "take away"; the
  read-aloud never says the key.

## 9. Records and scorecard

Device-local per child id (`?c=`), key `mf-g2-bar-models-v1:<child id>` (separate from the
test-prep key). Per session: the form counter, the position, each item's first-try result, tries,
highest rung, his answers, the matched rule, twins and look-again results, the paper item, the

check's points — counts only. The grown-up part lists each miss with his own answers and the
matched habit.

## 10. Check blueprint (`testing/bar_models_check.js`)

For every session (1–13) × 2,000 forms, and the check × fresh forms: 0 throws; every key recomputed
independently; every wrong choice and review value equals its named rule (recomputed in the
checker); every wrong sentence is false for the bar; the asked slot is empty (or "?") in every
pre-answer bar and bond; part widths in proportion and ≥ 18 %; one quantity per answer label; ≤ 10
words a sentence; stories 2 + 1; no key-word phrases; key place and rank ≤ 40 % (two-way ≤ 60 %);
no repeated tuple within a session; consecutive forms and extra practice share no tuple with the
earlier form; tier-1 and tier-2 exam values never appear (read at run time, counts only); the mask
covers every listed value; no G1-U02 setting; rungs 1–3 number-free, rung 1 a question, rung 4 the
answer; the page's grader accepts every key and rejects every rule value. **Broken controls** (each
must fire): a corrupted key; a pool value whose rule gives another number; a filled asked slot; an
11-word sentence; "altogether means add"; a fixture item carrying a tier-1 value built at run time
from the list; a mask with one listed value's bit cleared; a wrong sentence that is true.

**Added after the second teacher read (each with a control that fires):** read-aloud lines never tie
whole/part to a value or the ? (control: "The parts are 45 and 38."); a line names ?, bar, box or
circle only when on screen (controls: a story decision help naming the ?, a help naming the bar
before it is drawn); "left"/"more" on both sides per form (control: part-side "left" removed); the
bank — every setting on both sides, every frame's text equals its word tag, a plain frame for every
kind, both words on both sides (control: a setting with one side only) — plus per-setting share
15–85 % over all sessions; keys distinct within a session and twin/look-again keys never a key met
that day (controls: a twin with the shown key; two questions with one key); trades ≤ 1 on three-digit
items in sessions 2–6; the warm-up shape once per session on the first build question; sessions 4/5
end with the other kind; sessions 7/8 have 5 questions; the check totals 15 with no shape; the
add-back is not right with no answer and its rung 4 claims no sum; sessions 3 and 4 of one form share
no within-20 pair; within-20 step-ups counted.
