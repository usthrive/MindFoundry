## §1 Grade goals

**Common Core Grade 2** (E2): understand place value to 1,000 (2.NBT.A); add and subtract
fluently within 100, add up to four 2-digit numbers, add and subtract within 1,000, mentally
add or subtract 10 or 100, explain why strategies work (2.NBT.B); one- and two-step word
problems within 100 with unknowns in all positions, fluency within 20, equal groups and odd/even
(2.OA); standard length, time to five minutes, money, line plots and graphs (2.MD); halves,
thirds, fourths (2.G).

**Where the Singapore order runs earlier or later** (E11, E34, E36, E37): earlier — tables of
2–5 and 10 with division (P2; Common Core Grade 3), mass and volume (P2; Grade 3), time to the
minute and decimal money (P2; Grades 3–4), fractions of a whole (P2; Grade 3); later — bar graphs
(P3; Grade 2), and no line plots at all. The school's book keeps most of the Singapore order
(E26, E37) and drops Grade 2 fractions (E36). The module follows the school's book, labels the
above-grade units "school-beside", and treats bar graphs and line plots as core (E2).

**The mental-calculation gap.** Mental addition of two 2-digit numbers is P3 in Singapore but a
Grade 2 US standard, taught in the school's Ch 2 with number bonds (E82, E83). It is the first
lesson of U02, because it was his hidden miss (E236).

**The year's model work.** Blocks on a place-value chart to 1,000 (E117, E69), number bonds for
mental ± (E49), a counting tape for counting on (E87, E117), the ticked number line for rounding
(E117) and, from G2-U01 L7, for placing 3-digit numbers on a line to 1,000 (R5 C12, C14), the
open number line for jumps `[original design]`, and the bar model from Ch 4 — read
and labelled first, built by the end of U05, built in every later unit (DD6; E60, E61, E247).

## §4 Two expanded units

### §4.1 G2-U02 Addition within 1,000: the carry is a ten

**Teacher explanation covers** (short, elicit-not-narrate, L25): a number is made of hundreds,
tens and ones and each has its own column; ten of anything in a column is "too many" and
becomes one of the next column — that is the only rule, and it is the same rule for ones, tens
and for three or four addends; the little number over a column is **how many new tens** (or
hundreds) came out of the trade — usually 1, sometimes 2 or 3; a sum can also be done in the
head by jumps (keep one number whole, jump by tens, then ones) and the answer is the same; the
link to his Kumon-style columns is said once: "this trade is the little 1 you already write"
(METHOD-LEDGER B4, B5, B6). Every column is said with its **unit noun**, because only things with
the same noun are added: "8 ones and 8 ones make 16 ones; 16 ones are 1 ten and 6 ones", and the
child's explain rows use the same nouns (DD20; R5 C14, C6, C16). **Blocks for structure, facts
for answers:** the chart shows why the trade happens; once he has made two trades right on it,
the chart moves behind a "show me" button and each column is done from facts he knows; the
parent page says so in one line (R5 C14, C6). With three or four addends he looks for tens
inside the ones column first (R5 C14).

**Guided progression** (Steps 2–5 of the loop, per lesson; the rung used is recorded):
1. *Concrete.* The teacher shows one trade on the chart, asking "what do we have too many of?"
   Then the child drags ten ones into the tens column and they snap into a rod (N-I3). The
   target count is never shown (L33).
2. *Pictorial.* A picture of blocks in the pre-trade state (N-F9); the child says how many tens
   and ones after the trade, then fills the carry row first and the digits after (N-I1).
3. *Abstract.* The column alone with its carry row; then a mental item with the open number
   line (N-I6) and the strategy chips (N-I9); then "write the sentence".
4. *Guided practice* of 3–4 items with the help ladder: what it means (re-read with the item's

   nothing that names the move is highlighted) → use what you know (the chart appears, empty) → a way to finish (the ones
   column is traded for him; he does the rest) → the answer with the model filled.
5. *Explain* (one per lesson day): tap the true claims about a carry, then say the sentence

### §4.2 G2-U05 Part-whole bars

**Teacher explanation covers:** a story about one set that is split, joined or changed can be
drawn as **one bar**; the whole bar is the whole set, the pieces are the parts; the brace over
the bar (said to him as "the line over the whole bar", DD20) carries the whole; the "?" sits where the story's question is — on the brace if the
question is "how many in all / at first", inside the bar if it is "how many left / how many were
…"; a part is found by taking the other part from the whole, the whole by joining the parts —
the same idea as the bond with an empty part (U03); every bar ends in a number sentence on the
same screen (E229); at school he may be asked for the bar, here it is practised on its own and a
right answer is never taken away for skipping it (DD9).

**Guided progression** (the DD6 rungs climb with his first-try streak, never the calendar):
1. *Concrete.* Two sets of counters; a strip laid under both; the child pushes them together
   and says the whole (L1, N-I4).
2. *Read.* A bar is supplied with its numbers and a "?" (N-F4). "What does the bar say?" The
   child picks the matching sentence from four (T4). No decision yet.
3. *Label.* The bar's shape is given; the child drags the numbers and the "?" onto the parts and
   the brace (N-I5 step 3), then types the sentence (N-I8). Graded against the story's schema
   (code, never AI).
4. *Build.* No bar. The builder asks: how many things are we comparing (one set → one bar)?
   where do the numbers and "?" go? write the sentence. The tool draws; the child decides (DD7).
   After commit, the self-check shows his model beside the item's own with differences marked
   (E219). The item's model is never in view before commit (L33).
5. *Unaided and paper.* The last lesson ends with one paper item: "Draw this bar on paper, then
   type the number sentence you wrote under it" — recorded as "paper practice done" (DD8).

**A lesson day's anatomy** (N = 8; DD1): two starter items (L1: a bond with an empty part
within 20; L2–L5: a bar read from the lesson before), four through the steps above, two exit
items on the lesson's key point (L3: where does the "?" go?). Every story runs the **story
routine** of DD17: it is read aloud → he says the story back by tapping who and what it is
about → he taps what is asked → he labels the model → the answer is checked in the story. If he
says a key word as a rule ("left means take away"), the teacher says "check it on the bar"; the
bar decides, never the word (DD17; E211).

**A practice day's anatomy** (N = 10): seven new items — two read, two label, two build, one

positions (E167 Rec. 5); stories of at most two sentences and one question, read aloud (DD17).
