## §1 Grade goals

**Common Core Grade 4** (E4): place value to 1,000,000, rounding to any place, multi-digit × and ÷
with 1-digit divisors (4.NBT); the four operations in multi-step problems, multiplicative
comparison, factors and multiples, patterns (4.OA); fraction equivalence and ordering, like-
denominator ± including mixed numbers, fraction × whole, decimal notation for tenths and hundredths
(4.NF); conversions from a larger unit to a smaller, area and perimeter formulas, line plots, angle
measure (4.MD); lines, angles, classification of shapes, symmetry (4.G).

**Where the Singapore order runs earlier or later** (E13, E14, E34): earlier — ± of fractions with
two different denominators (P4; 5.NF.1), decimals to three places with ± and × ÷ by a 1-digit whole
(P4; Grade 5), nets (P4; Grade 6); later — fraction × whole (P5; 4.NF.4), and Singapore's number
range stops at 100,000 (P4) where Common Core reaches 1,000,000. Grade 4 is the school book's
best-aligned year (E26): only single items run above grade. The module teaches the Common Core
year as core, treats the Singapore-early parts as stretch (§5), and places the lineage's Grade 4 bar
work — before-after and the algebra type — in the module-own U03 (E251, E255, E256).

**The year's model work.** Objects change: **place-value disks** replace blocks (E68, E69, E119) —
the value is in the label and the column, not the size, so a chart reaches 1,000,000 and crosses the
point to 0.1 and 0.01. The area model gains four partial products (E119), and disks shared column
by column become long division (METHOD-LEDGER D5, D6). Bars: pupils are usually given the model in
Grade 4 (E61); the module goes one rung higher on purpose — a **new bar type is shown once at first
exposure, then built** (DD6, DD19; E64): the multiplicative-comparison bar, the before-after bar and
the sum-and-difference bar (core now, DD12; E256). Fraction bars with equal pieces and the bar for a
fraction of a set carry the fraction work (E119). Heuristics new this grade: before-after,
tabulating, simplify the problem (E268).

**Formats and explaining.** Grade 4 checks carry the Grade 3 formats and the Grade 5 additions
(DD19): one explain-a-claim choice item, fill-in grids, yes/no rows, judge-and-explain, explain an
error, a word problem with a built model or shown steps, multiple select. The reason frame is typed;
the tap step earns the point (DD16).

## §4 Two expanded units

### §4.1 G4-U02 Multiplication and division

**Teacher explanation covers** (short, elicit-not-narrate, L25): a disk's value is in its label and
its column, not its size — ten of any disk make one of the next, in both directions; multiplying by
10 moves every disk one column left, so 30 × 40 is 3 tens × 4 tens = 12 hundreds; a 2-digit × 2-digit
product is **four** rectangles, because each part of one number meets each part of the other, and the
column method is those four products added two at a time (METHOD-LEDGER D5: "your Kumon rows are
those four products added two at a time"); division is sharing disks one column at a time, from the
largest — if a column has nothing to share, the quotient still gets a 0 in that place, and a leftover
is traded down and shared with the next column (D6: "bring down means move to the next column");
estimate first by simplifying the numbers, so a wrong answer feels wrong; a remainder means
something — the question says whether to round up, drop it, or keep it; and every division is
checked by quotient × divisor + remainder.

**Guided progression:**
1. *Concrete.* Disks on the chart (N-I3). The teacher shows one trade and one shift; then the child
   shifts disks for × 10 and shares disks for ÷ by dragging into groups. The target count is never
   shown (L33).
2. *Pictorial, the child builds.* The area model on the grid (N-I13): the child cuts the rectangle
   into tens and ones both ways and writes a product in each box. For division, a picture of the
   pre-share state of each column (N-F2 pre-trade only).
3. *Abstract.* The column with its two rows, each row linked by colour to its boxes; the written
   division beside the shared disks, one step per column, the 0 written when a column shares
   nothing.
4. *Guided practice* of 3–4 items with the help ladder: what it means (re-read with "groups" or
   "shared" highlighted) → use what you know (an empty grid or chart appears) → a way to finish
   (the first product or the first share is done; he does the rest) → the answer with the model
   filled. No rung below 4 states the answer.
5. *Explain* (one per lesson day): tap the true claims, then type the sentence (N-I10): "The 0 in
   104 means no tens were shared, because 2 tens cannot be shared into 6 groups."

**A practice day's anatomy** (N = 10; DD1, DD10): "Question k of 10" on every screen. Seven new
items from the lesson just taught and the one before it, mixed by rule (E90): one × 10/100 item, one
2-digit × 2-digit with the grid, one in the column, one 3-digit ÷ 1-digit with a 0 in the quotient and
one without, one remainder story with the question deciding, one estimate-then-exact item. Three
returns from the weakest skills (e.g. U01 L4 factor pairs, G3-U03 across zeros, G3-U06 a two-step
chain). Help open, costed, recorded; a miss says what is right and where to look (TAD §3.10); a blank
is "not yet" (TAD §3.19). A rest after question 10, never a stop. Speed recorded, never shown (DD13).

### §4.2 G4-U03 Bar models: four operations, comparison, before-after, the algebra type

**Teacher explanation covers:** "times as many" is not "more than" — "3 times as many" makes one bar
of **three equal units** beside a bar of one unit, and "3 more than" makes a gap of 3; when the total
of both bars is known, **count every unit first** (1 + 3 = 4), then share the total; a story that
changes something and tells you the end is drawn **twice**, before and after, and the start is found
by undoing each change in reverse order — the before-after heuristic (E268); two numbers from their
sum and their difference are two bars with a **gap** — take the gap off the sum and the two bars are
equal, so share what is left, then **check both clues** (E235); the sentence is the bar written out
(E229); solve items earn the mark by any sound method, and the model is practised on its own (DD9);
and the links once: "same bars as Ms. Wren, but now each box is one unit — count the units first"
(METHOD-LEDGER C6), "this puzzle is two bars with a gap" (C5).

**Guided progression** (the Grade 4 rung, DD6: a new bar type is **shown once**, then built):
1. *Shown once.* At the first multiplicative-comparison story only, two bars appear, one of one unit
   and one of three equal units, the unit value in the short bar and "?" on the long one (N-F4; T4):
   "what does this bar say?" The same one-time rung opens the before-after bar in L3. The
   sum-and-difference bar is not shown: the child built it as stretch in Grade 2 (G2-U07-S1).
2. *Build.* No bar. The builder asks: how many things are we comparing (two)? is the comparison
   "times" or "more than" (equal units or a gap)? how many units in each bar? where do the numbers
   and "?" go? write the sentence (N-I5; N-I8). The tool draws; the child decides (DD7). Bars are
   drawn equal until he says which is longer (L51). Self-check after commit (E219).
3. *Before-after.* Two bar rows, "before" and "after"; the child places the known end on "after",
   the changes as parts, and "?" on "before"; the undo steps are written in order.
4. *Check both clues.* On sum-and-difference items the check step is a `choices` row: "do the two
   numbers add to the sum?" "do they differ by the difference?" — both must be tapped before Check.
5. *Unaided and paper.* L5 ends with the paper item (N-I11; DD8).

**A practice day's anatomy** (N = 10): seven new items — two multiplicative-comparison builds (unit
known; total known), one additive comparison so the "times or more than" decision stays live (E90),
one before-after build, one sum-and-difference build with the two-clue check, one solve item by any
method with the optional "show it as a bar too" (DD9), one T12 fictional wrong model (a "times" story
drawn as a gap). Three returns (e.g. U02 L5 a division with a 0 in the quotient, U01 L3 rounding,
G3-U07 L7 a fraction of a set). Stories of at most three sentences and one question; read aloud on
tap (DD17).

## §6 Entry and exit profile

**Entry (what a child needs to begin Grade 4 units — equals the Grade 3 exit in GRADE-3.md §6).**
Builds 4-digit numbers on a chart and as a bond; rounds to 10 and 100 on a number line and says
which digit decides; adds and subtracts within 10,000 in a column, across zeros, with an estimate to
check; adds and subtracts two 2-digit numbers mentally and names the route; sees a ×6–×9 fact as a
split array and uses the fact family to divide, with a remainder named as leftover; multiplies 2- and
3-digit numbers by a 1-digit number through the area model and the stacked column; **builds unaided**
part-whole, comparison, equal-unit and two-step bars for stories in any of the four operations, and
the bar for a fraction of a set; names, places on a number line, compares and finds equivalents for
fractions; reads scales and jugs and solves measurement stories with bars; finds area by tiles and by
rows × columns, perimeter and a missing side; tells time to the minute and finds elapsed time on an
open line; makes and reads scaled graphs and line plots; checks a right angle, tells parallel from
perpendicular, sorts quadrilaterals with a typed reason. Checked by each unit's entry check (DD3). A
child new to disks meets one trade with blocks beside disks in U01 L1 (METHOD-LEDGER A5 link).

**Exit (what "knows" looks like at the end of Grade 4, per skill, two days, first try, no scaffold;
METHODOLOGY §4).** Builds numbers to 1,000,000 with disks, rounds to any place and says a digit is ten
times the one to its right; finds factor pairs and multiples, tells prime from composite, continues a
pattern from a rule; multiplies 2-digit × 2-digit through the four-box area model and the column,
multiplies 4-digit × 1-digit, divides 3- and 4-digit numbers by a 1-digit number with disks then the
written method, estimates first, checks with × and +, and says what a remainder means; **builds**
multiplicative-comparison, before-after and sum-and-difference bars and checks both clues; makes
equivalent fractions and simplest form, compares with like pieces, shows mixed and improper
fractions, adds and subtracts like denominators across a whole, multiplies a fraction by a whole and
finds a fraction of a set with a bar; shows tenths and hundredths with disks and the hundred square,
compares decimals, writes fourths and halves as decimals; converts larger to smaller units with a
table and a bar; uses the area and perimeter formulas, finds a missing side and a composite area;
measures and draws angles and adds angle parts on a bar; classifies triangles and quadrilaterals with
a typed reason and draws lines of symmetry; builds tables and line plots with fractions and reads a
line graph. Stretch state shown separately.

## §7 Band notes

- **Reading load (DD17).** Stories: at most 14 words a sentence, three sentences of story, one
  question; two-step stories may carry a fourth sentence. Story words per unit ("times as many",
  "at first", "then", "shared equally", "in all", "per", "of"). No key-word teaching (E211). A miss
  on a long story where his answer shows he did not parse the task is flagged **reading** (TAD §3.15).
- **Read-aloud.** On tap (DD17), including checks; `spoken` never carries the asked value
  (METHODOLOGY §6.3).
- **Explain items (DD16).** Typed reason frame; the tap rows earn the point; the typed sentence is
  read formatively (TAD §7). Reasoning words this grade: "ten times", "partial product", "the
  quotient needs a 0", "remainder", "unit", "equal units", "like pieces", "tenths", "hundredths",
  "perimeter is the edge".
- **Touch and keyboard (HANDOFF §6).** Every builder works with a finger and with arrow keys + Enter.
  Inputs this grade needs beyond METHODOLOGY §6 and GRADE-3.md §7 `[original design]`, each under the
  picture-kind gate and L33, each graded by code, each with a broken-control gate (HANDOFF §12):
  **N-I17 long-division steps** (one step per column; the 0 must be typed where a column shares
  nothing; the carry-down drawn by the tool after the child names the column); **N-I18 protractor
  set** (the child rotates the protractor to the arm's 0 and reads; the guess typed first and
  recorded, L41); **N-I19 fold tool** for symmetry (the halves overlay after the child draws the
  line); the bar builder N-I5 gains a **before / after** row pair and a **"times or more than"**
  decision; the place-value chart N-I3 renders **disks** (N-F2) from this grade, with 0.1 and 0.01
  columns in U05. Digit boxes highlight nothing until tapped (TAD §3.17). A bar is never resized by
  dragging an edge (DD7). Check is never locked; a blank is "not yet" (TAD §3.19). Tap targets at
  least 48 px.
- **Screens.** "Question k of N" on every screen; one claim per row; typed answers state their shape
  with numbers unrelated to the item ("e.g. 11 × 12 = 132", "e.g. 2 1/5"), never the item's own
  numbers or key (METHODOLOGY §6.3); error-analysis items show the fix step before the claim rows
  (METHODOLOGY §2 Step 7); no timers shown (TAD §4, §3.5). Multiple-select items say
  "select every …" and grade each pick (one claim per row).
- **Pictures.** Every figure is the kind of thing its words name (TAD §3.21): disks are disks, not
  blocks (E69); a protractor is a protractor; a grid is a grid. Where the child answers, nothing in the
  picture is countable or readable in place of the asked step (L33): disks in the pre-trade state only
