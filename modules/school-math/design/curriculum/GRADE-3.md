## §1 Grade goals

**Common Core Grade 3** (E3): multiply and divide within 100 and understand the properties and the
× ÷ relationship (3.OA); round to 10 and 100, add and subtract fluently within 1,000, multiply a
1-digit number by a multiple of 10 (3.NBT); understand fractions as numbers — unit fractions, the
number line, equivalence, comparison (3.NF); time intervals, liquid volume and mass, scaled graphs
and line plots, area as tiling and as rows × columns, perimeter (3.MD); shapes by attribute and
equal-area partitions (3.G).

**Where the Singapore order runs earlier or later** (E4, E12, E34, E35): earlier — numbers and ± to
10,000 with 4-digit algorithms (P3; Common Core puts 4-digit ± fluency at 4.NBT.4), 3-digit × 1-digit
(P3; 4.NBT.5), simplest form and ± of related fractions (P3; Grade 4–5), compound units and
conversions (P3; 4.MD.1), angles and perpendicular / parallel lines (P3; 4.G.1); later — rounding
(P4; Grade 3 in Common Core). The school's book keeps most of the Singapore order, so its Ch 2–3
tests are mostly 4-digit ±, Ch 5 reaches 4-digit × 1-digit, Ch 8 carries conversions and Ch 12 is
partly 4.G.2 (E26). The module teaches each of these where the school does, flags the part that
Common Core places in Grade 4 as **school-beside** in the lesson row, and keeps the Common Core
part as core. Rounding is taught in U01 on a ticked number line because the school's Ch 1 does
(E118), with the parent told it is Grade 3 work in Common Core.

**The year's model work.** Counters on a place-value chart to 10,000 (E118) — the last year of
blocks and counters; disks arrive in Grade 4 (E69). Number bonds split a factor for × (E49, E118).
Arrays, the area model and square tiles are one picture seen three ways (E118). Folded strips and
the ticked number line make fractions numbers (E118). **From this grade the child builds every bar
by default** (DD6; E247, E62): part-whole and comparison bars with 4-digit numbers, equal-unit bars
for × and ÷, two-step chains, and the bar for a fraction of a set (E118, E244). A supplied bar
appears only at the first exposure of a new type (METHODOLOGY §3 invariant 3).

**Reading and explaining.** Read-aloud moves to on-tap (DD17). The reason frame is typed from this
grade; the point is still earned by the tap step (DD16). The chapter check gains the Grade 3 formats
of E259 and E130.

## §4 Two expanded units

### §4.1 G3-U06 Bar models: the four operations

**Teacher explanation covers** (short, elicit-not-narrate, L25): every story has a whole and parts,
and the bar is a picture of that, so the first question is always "how many things are we
comparing?" — one set split is one bar, two sets compared are two bars; a story about **equal groups
is a new kind of bar**: one unit for each group, the same number inside every unit, the brace over all
of them; multiplication fills the brace from the units, division fills a unit from the brace
(sharing) or counts the units (grouping) — the same picture read three ways, like the array in U04;
a two-step story is two bars, and the first bar's answer becomes a number in the second; a leftover
in a ÷ story is a short last unit, and the question says what to do with it; every bar ends in a
number sentence (E229); and the links said once: "you have seen bars on Ms. Wren's pages; here you

**Guided progression** (the DD6 rungs climb with the first-try streak, never the calendar; the rung

1. *Concrete.* Counters in equal groups; the child acts the story out (act it out) — 6 cups, 4 in

4. *Chain.* The plan step first ("first I find …", `choices`); bar 1 built; its answer dragged into
   bar 2; two sentences.
5. *Unaided and paper.* L7 ends with the paper item: "Draw this bar on paper. Then type the number
   sentence you wrote under it" — recorded as "paper practice done" (DD8).

### §4.2 G3-U07 Fractions as numbers

**Teacher explanation covers:** a fraction is a **number**, not a picture of food — it is born when
one whole is cut into **equal** parts and one part is named; the bottom number says how many equal
parts the whole was cut into, the top counts how many of them we have; a fraction is built from
unit pieces (3/4 is three 1/4 pieces, so 5/4 is five of them and is more than one whole); on a
number line the whole from 0 to 1 is cut into equal **spaces**, and the fraction is the point after
that many jumps; two fractions are equivalent when the strips end at the same place — cutting every
piece in half doubles the top and the bottom and changes nothing; to compare, same bottom → count
the tops, same top → more parts means smaller pieces; "a fraction **of** a set" is a bar cut into
equal units with the set on the brace (E118, E244) — the same equal-unit bar as U06, read with a
fraction; and the links once: "a fraction is one of the equal parts you folded; on the Kumon sheet
you only write its name" (METHOD-LEDGER E1), "you have seen fraction pictures with Ms. Wren; here
you fold and place them" (E1, E2).

**Guided progression:**
1. *Concrete.* The child folds a strip (on screen, the strip-fold input; at home, paper, L1) into 2,
   then 4, then 3 equal parts, and shades one part. Unequal folds are allowed and then tested:
   "are the parts equal?"
2. *Pictorial, the child builds.* The strip with unnumbered parts (N-F7); the child shades a given
   fraction, or names a shaded one. Then the number line: the child cuts 0–1 into equal spaces and
   places the fraction (ticked line, built). Then two strips laid under each other for equivalence.
3. *Abstract.* The fraction written; "which is greater?" with the reason frame typed (DD16):
   "1/8 is smaller than 1/3 because eighths are smaller pieces than thirds."
4. *The bar for a fraction of a set.* First exposure: 20 counters shared into 4 equal circles
   (N-I4); then the equal-unit bar built with the set on the brace and one unit asked (N-I5); then
   3 units.
5. *Guided practice* of 3–4 items with the help ladder: what it means (re-read with "equal parts"
   highlighted) → use what you know (an empty strip appears) → a way to finish (the first fold is
   made; he does the rest) → the answer with the strip filled. No rung below 4 states the answer.
6. *Explain* (one per lesson day): tap the true claims, then type the sentence (N-I10).

**A practice day's anatomy** (N = 10): seven new items — two name-the-fraction from an unnumbered
strip, one number-line placement, one equivalence pair with strips, one comparison with the reason
frame (tap rows graded), one fraction-of-a-set bar built, one solve item by any method. Three returns
(e.g. U06 L3 a sharing bar, U05 L3 a 3-digit × 1-digit column, U03 L4 across zeros). Pictures: the
strip's parts are never numbered on "what fraction" items; the number line shows the ticks the child
made, never the answer label (L33).

## §6 Entry and exit profile

**Entry (what a child needs to begin Grade 3 units — equals the Grade 2 exit in GRADE-2.md §6).**
Builds 3-digit numbers on a chart and as a bond; adds and subtracts within 1,000 in a column with
the trades understood and the carry counted (2 or 3 when needed); adds two 2-digit numbers mentally
and names the route; finds a missing addend or a missing digit through the part-whole view; finds
and explains a place-value error in the words "tens" and "ones"; **builds** a part-whole bar, a
comparison bar and a two-step chain for a story with the unknown anywhere, and writes the sentence;
measures in cm, m, in, ft; reads and makes graphs and line plots; tells time to five minutes; counts
money; partitions shapes two ways. Checked by each unit's entry check, never by a grade test (DD3).
A child whose Grade 2 bar skills read "learning" starts U06 one rung lower (label), and the rung
climbs by streak (DD6).

**Exit (what "knows" looks like at the end of Grade 3, per skill, two days, first try, no scaffold;
METHODOLOGY §4).** Builds 4-digit numbers on a chart and as a bond; rounds to 10 and 100 on a
number line and says which digit decides; adds and subtracts within 10,000 in a column, across
zeros, with an estimate to check; adds and subtracts two 2-digit numbers mentally and names the
route; sees a ×6–×9 fact as a split array and uses the fact family to divide, with a remainder
named as leftover; multiplies 2- and 3-digit numbers by a 1-digit number through the area model and
the stacked column; **builds unaided** part-whole, comparison, equal-unit and two-step bars for
stories in any of the four operations, and the bar for a fraction of a set; names, places on a
number line, compares and finds equivalents for fractions; reads scales and jugs and solves
measurement stories with bars; finds area by tiles and by rows × columns, perimeter and a missing
side; tells time to the minute and finds elapsed time on an open line; makes and reads scaled graphs
and line plots; checks a right angle, tells parallel from perpendicular, sorts quadrilaterals with
a typed reason. Stretch state shown separately. School-beside parts (4-digit ±, 4-digit × 1-digit,
conversions, angles and lines) are recorded as "learning / knows" like any skill but flagged Grade
4 on the parent page.

## §7 Band notes

- **Reading load (DD17).** Stories: at most 12 words a sentence, three sentences of story, one
  question. Story words listed per unit and said once with a picture ("shared equally", "in each",
  "times", "left over", "how much more", "of the set"). No key-word teaching (E211). A miss on a long
  story where his answer shows he did not parse the task is flagged **reading**, not maths (TAD §3.15).
- **Read-aloud.** On tap from Grade 3 (DD17), including checks; `spoken` never carries the asked
  value (METHODOLOGY §6.3). The parent may turn it back on by default.
- **Explain items (DD16).** From this grade the child **types** the reason frame; the point is earned
  by the tap rows, which stay on every explain item; the typed sentence is read formatively from the
  item's own rubric (TAD §7) and shown to the parent. Reasoning words expected this grade: "regroup",
  "a hundred is ten tens", "equal units", "the brace is the whole", "equal parts", "a unit fraction".
- **Touch and keyboard (HANDOFF §6).** Every builder works with a finger and with arrow keys + Enter.
  Inputs this grade needs beyond METHODOLOGY §6 `[original design]`, each under the picture-kind gate
  and L33, each graded by code, each with a broken-control gate (HANDOFF §12): **N-I12 strip-fold /
  equal-parts input** (fold into n parts; shade k; parts never numbered where the fraction is asked);
  **N-I13 grid-tile / area-model input** (drag tiles; cut a rectangle; the count is never shown while
  the child covers the shape); **N-I14 corner-and-ruler tool** (lay a right-angle corner; measure a gap
  in two places); **N-I15 clock-hands set** and **N-I16 graph builder** are now METHODOLOGY §6.2
  (first used in Grade 1) and gain to-the-minute hands and a chosen key or scale here; the strip-fold
  input N-I12 allows unequal folds and then counts parts as equal when their lengths differ by no
  more than one snap step (1/24 of the strip) `[original design]`; the bar builder N-I5 gains an **equal-unit choice** (how many units; unit value or
  unit count asked) and a **short last unit** for remainders. Digit boxes highlight nothing until
  tapped (TAD §3.17). A bar is never resized by dragging an edge (DD7). Check is never locked; a
  blank is "not yet" (TAD §3.19). Tap targets at least 48 px.
- **Screens.** "Question k of N" on every screen; one claim per row on explain items; typed answers
  state their shape with numbers unrelated to the item ("e.g. 2 × 11 = 22", "e.g. 1/5"), never the
  item's own numbers or key (METHODOLOGY §6.3); error-analysis items show the fix step before the
  claim rows (METHODOLOGY §2 Step 7); no timers shown (TAD §4, §3.5).
- **Pictures.** Every figure is the kind of thing its words name (TAD §3.21): a strip is a strip, a
  grid is a grid, counters on a chart are counters, a bar is a bar. Where the child answers, nothing
  in the picture is countable in place of the asked step (L33): pre-trade states only on regroup
  items; unnumbered parts on "what fraction" strips; the tile count hidden while the child covers an
  area; equal bars until he decides which is longer; on equal-unit bars the item's unit count is
  never drawn before he chooses it.
