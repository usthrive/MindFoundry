## §4 Two expanded units

### §4.1 G1-U02 Number bonds; + and − within 10

**Teacher explanation covers** (L25: ask, do not tell): a number can be split into two parts,
and the picture of that is the **number bond** — one circle for the whole, two for the parts;
the same bond tells four stories (two adds, two take-aways); adding puts parts together, taking
away leaves a part, and "find the missing part" is the same picture with one circle empty; the
equal sign says "is the same as", so 7 = 3 + 4 is a true sentence. The vocabulary rule is said
once: "in this program a number bond is this picture; the facts you know by heart are called
facts" (DD14; E200). The link to his Kumon-style facts, if he has them, is METHOD-LEDGER B1.

**Guided progression:**
1. *Concrete.* The teacher splits 6 counters once and asks "how else?" The child drags counters
   into two circles (N-I4) and lists the splits (make a list).
2. *Pictorial.* The child builds the bond: taps which circle is the whole, enters the numbers
   (N-I2 build). On a "find the part" item the asked circle is empty and the other two are filled
   (N-F1 rule).
3. *Abstract.* The sentence built from chips (N-I8), then the four facts of one bond, then
   true / false sentences one per row.

### §4.2 G1-U08 + and − within 40

**Teacher explanation covers:** tens and ones live in their own columns; when the ones column
