## DD1 — The unit is a chapter-aligned set of lessons; a day is a question count `[original design]`

- **Gap.** No item-per-day count exists for Singapore-style books (E107); the school's chapters
  run 15–23 school days each (E139); the daily dose for this module is the owner's open decision
  (O3).
- **Default.** The unit of the curriculum is a **unit**: one topic, aligned to one school chapter
  (or part of one), made of **lessons** (one idea each) and closed by a **chapter check**. A unit
  has no fixed number of days. A **day** is a question count, not a clock: the child does N
  questions, where N is the owner's O3 setting. Recommended start, until records say otherwise:
  **N = 8** on a lesson day and **N = 10** on a practice day; the app offers a rest after N, never a
  stop (tutoring-app-design §3.11). Weeks are not a unit here: the school moves by chapter, and
  the Best Brains-style module already owns the weekly rhythm (HANDOFF §7).
- **Basis.** The school's own structure is chapter → section → lesson part (E109, E110); a Math
  in Focus lesson is one idea taught three ways with a short guided and a short independent set
  (E113, E103). Chunking by chapter lets the parent map the module to what came home. The
  question-count rule is the tutoring-app-design law (§3.11).
- **Overturned by.** The owner's O3 ruling (which simply sets N); records showing N too high
  (first-try accuracy falling across a day) or too low (every day ends with the rest offer
  declined).
- **Note for the owner's O3 ruling** `[original design]` (REVIEW-2026-11-17 A6). He already does
  Kumon-style work (about 30 minutes, E106) and the Best Brains-style day. A lesson day's teacher
  demo, chart build and model build (Steps 2–3) and the explain item (Step 7) take time that N
  does not count, so N = 8 on a lesson day may run 25–30 minutes. The recommendation to the owner
  is therefore to **start at N = 5–6 on lesson days, with the lesson steps counted in N, and the
  explain item on alternate lesson days**, then adjust from his records (the "overturned by" line
  above). The owner decides.
- **Lesson-day anatomy** `[original design]` (R5 C12, ledger E287; added 2026-11-18). A lesson day
  opens with two **starter** items on the lesson's prerequisites (drawn from the unit's Entry
  check line) and closes with two **exit** items on the lesson's one key point, unaided; the
  items between them run the lesson's steps. N stays as set (8 recommended; with N = 5–6, one
  starter and two exit). Starter and exit are labelled in the record, so lesson-day data shows
  whether the lesson landed before practice days begin (DD10); a starter miss is a running
  readiness signal (DD3), and neither ever enters the mastery count (METHODOLOGY §4).

## DD2 — The module keeps its own sequence, keyed to the school's chapters, not its lessons `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Official lesson titles for Grades 1, 3–5 are not public (G17); copying a full
  lesson-by-lesson sequence with its titles carries a residual legal risk (E154).
- **Default.** Each unit names the **school chapter** it sits beside (a lookup, e.g. "beside the
  school's Grade 2 chapter on subtraction within 1,000") and the **Common Core cluster** it serves.
  The module's internal lesson order is its own. Units never carry the publisher's lesson titles.
- **Basis.** Chapter titles and topic order may be stated; pages, items and compilations may not
  be copied (E152, E153). The Common Core spine is public and fixed (E1–E5). Districts reorder
  Book B freely (E38), so a lesson-level key would break anyway.
- **Overturned by.** Nothing likely; this is a legal posture, not a learning claim.

## DD3 — Entry: from the misses, then a short check; two same-structure items before a label `[original design]`

- **Gap.** No public placement test exists **for Math in Focus** in Grades 1–2, and the
  publisher's Transition Guide is not public (E122, G19). *Corrected 2026-11-18 (R5 C1–C5; ledger
  P3.1, E270):* free Grade 1–2 placement tests do exist for the sister Singapore programmes
  (Dimensions Math 1A–2B; Primary Mathematics 2022 readiness tests for 2A and 2B); none exists for
  Math in Focus, so the module still writes its own. The owner's O5 default is "from the misses,
  then a short check".
- **Default.** A child enters at a **unit**, not a grade. For the first learner the entry point is
  fixed by his test reading (E231–E241): the first units are the Grade 2 entry plan (DD5). Every
  unit opens with an **entry check** of 4–8 items on its prerequisites, modelled on the school's
  per-chapter pre-check (E109, E122). Items come in **pairs of the same structure with different
  numbers**; a gap is labelled only when both items in a pair miss. A clean entry check lets the
  child skip that unit's concrete step and go straight to its practice; a failed pair opens the
  prerequisite lesson first. **Each unit's entry check is specified in its grade file**, as an
  "Entry check" line under the unit's lesson table: two or three prerequisite skills, each served
  as a pair and each naming the lesson it comes from (the lesson a failed pair opens).
  **A child entering at Grades 3–5** (a sibling, a new family) takes a **grade-entry probe** on the
  pattern of the Grade 2 probe (DD4): six pairs drawn from the grade's entry profile (each grade
  file's §6), read aloud at Grades 1–2, no feedback until the end. The child starts at the first
  unit of the grade whose entry-check pairs the probe did not clear; a clean probe starts at U01.
  **Two kinds of check, named** (R5 C1; ledger E271): an entry check and a probe are **readiness**
  checks ("is he ready to begin this?"); DD24's short review of the last chapter finished is a
  **mastery** check ("did he learn what was taught?"). The parent page uses these two words.
  **How a probe is served** (R5 C1, C5; ledger E272, E273): the warm items around each pair are
  ordered as a **difficulty ladder** (no trade → one trade → two trades, or the grade's own
  steps) so the parent sees where the child breaks, not only whether; untimed (no clock shown,
  none used to judge, DD13); unscored ("holds / does not hold / unclear"), with the errors
  watched; and **short**: a publisher's half-grade placement test is about twenty questions and an
  hour on paper, too long for a screen at 7, so the module's probe stays at six pairs plus its
  warm items, over two days.
- **Basis.** One wrong answer is a repair at an impasse, not a stable rule (E77). Mix items that
  need the skill with items that do not, so the check also tests discrimination (E90).
- **Overturned by.** A returned school test showing a skill the entry check called known.

## DD4 — The regrouping probe `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Which regrouping rule(s) the first learner holds is unknown (G7): his Q2 fits two rules
  (E232); Q3 and Q5b share "each column alone" (E233, E234).
- **Default.** The first entry check is a **probe of six item pairs**, each pair isolating one
  candidate rule, with original numbers (never the test's items — DECISIONS M6):
  1. four 2-digit addends whose ones make 20–29 (does the carry come out as 2?);
  2. three 1-digit addends past 20 (same question, smaller load);
  3. a missing addend with regrouping, shown as a column (does "each column alone" appear?);
  4. the same missing addend shown as a number bond with an empty part;
  5. missing digits in a column where only the presence of a carry differs between the pair;
  6. a 2-digit sum with "do it in your head" and a tap-to-say "which way did you do it?" (no
     column surface offered).
  Each pair's result goes to the parent as "holds / does not hold / unclear", never as a score.
  **Warm block** (2026-11-18; R5 C14, C5, C6): one **facts pair** first — two one-digit sums that
  cross ten (a ones-column total in the teens) — then the **warm ladder**: a 2-digit sum with no
  trade → one with one trade → a 3-digit sum with two trades. A child who misses the facts pair
  needs fact supply (the Kumon-style sheet range, DD13), not a regrouping lesson; the ladder
  shows where the trade breaks. The Grade 2 instance is GRADE-2 §0.2 (seventeen items, two days).
  **Reading note on pair 1, rule B (ones mis-added):** the repair is the ones-column bond and
  "look for tens first inside the ones column" (G2-U02 L6; R5 C14), not a new carry rule.
- **Basis.** Bugs are systematic and migrate over days, so one answer cannot fix a rule (E77);
  the carry is "how many new tens", not always 1 (E232, E75); the part-whole gap, not borrowing,
  is the Q3 miss (E233); the mental item is a hidden miss (E236).
- **Overturned by.** The probe itself: whichever rules it finds, the first lessons target those.

## DD5 — The Grade 2 entry plan has no dates and works in any chapter order `[original design]`

- **Gap.** The school's own calendar, current chapter and Book B order are unknown (G1, owner
  item); the best guess is Ch 3 now, Ch 4 from late November into January (E139, E141).
- **Default.** The entry plan is an **ordered list of units with no dates**:
  1. the meaning of the carry (place-value trade with blocks on a chart; three and four addends);
  2. part-whole with an unknown part (bond → missing addend → + and − as inverses);
  3. subtraction within 1,000 as the mirror (regroup tens, hundreds, across zeros), beside the
     school's Ch 3;
  4. part-whole bars (supplied → labelled → built), beside Ch 4.1–4.2;
  5. comparison bars, beside Ch 4.3;
  6. two-step problems with bars, beside Ch 4.4;
  7. stretch: two numbers from their sum and difference (DD12).
  The parent can drag a unit forward when the teacher's note names a chapter. Pre-teaching a
  model one unit **before** the class meets it is the intended default.
- **Basis.** Pre-teaching a representation lowers load at first exposure (E63, E228); the Ch 4
  section order is part-whole → adding on / taking away → comparison → two-step (E60); the
  comparison bar his Q7 needed is taught after the test he took (E142); Book B varies by
  district (E38).
- **Overturned by.** The owner's note of the real calendar (closes G1).

## DD6 — Build or read a model: the ladder by grade `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Whether Grade 2 lessons ask the child to label or to size bars (G2, one district only,
  E62); no study compares screen and paper at ages 6–8 (E179).
- **Default.** Every representation walks the same **four rungs**: **read** (the model is shown and
  the child reads a number off it) → **label** (the model's shape is given; the child places the
  numbers and the "?") → **build** (the child chooses the model type, how many bars, which is
  longer, where the brace goes; the tool snaps the drawing) → **draw unaided** (no tool help, on
  screen; and on paper per DD8). The grade at which each representation reaches "build" is fixed
  in METHODOLOGY-MODEL §3. In short: **number bonds, ten frames and blocks on a chart are built from
  Grade 1**; **bars are read and labelled in the first Grade 2 bar unit and built by the end of
  Grade 2**; **from Grade 3 the child builds every bar by default**; **in Grade 4 a new bar type
  (before-after, algebra-type) may be supplied once at first exposure, then built**; **in Grade 5
  the child builds and names each bar's unit with a letter**. Within a unit the rung climbs with
  the child's first-try streak, never with the calendar, and the rung used is recorded per item.
- **Basis.** The school hands over the model grade by grade: none in Grade 1 → supplied in Grade
  2 lessons, completed on the Ch 4 test, drawn on the Ch 6 test → drawn in Grade 3 lessons →
  usually given again in Grade 4 (E247, E243–E246, E61, E129). Fading from supplied to child-built
  is the method's own rule (E59). Children judge given diagrams better than they draw them, so
  recognising is not knowing (E64). A picture only looked at is "skip", not CPA (E42). Guided
  construction is the praised pattern; fill-the-blank is criticised (E224). Rungs recorded per
  item are the tutoring-app-design help-ladder law (§3.2, §3.3).
- **Overturned by.** The Teacher Edition's own progression pages (teacher-only, E155); records
  showing the "build" rung too hard at Grade 2 (first-try accuracy on build items far below label
  items after the fade).

## DD7 — Building a bar on screen: guided construction, the tool draws, the child decides `[original design]`

- **Gap.** How a 7-year-old builds a bar model on a screen is undocumented (G8); no reviewed app
  resizes bars by dragging their ends (E227).
- **Default.** The bar builder asks the child the **decisions** and does the **drawing**:
  1. "How many things are we comparing?" → one bar (part-whole) or two bars (comparison);
  2. "Which is more?" → the child taps the bar that should be longer; the tool sizes it roughly;
  3. "Where does each number go?" → the child drags numbers and the "?" onto bar parts, the brace
     or the difference gap;
  4. "Write the number sentence" → typed or tapped; the model is complete only when the sentence
     matches the model.
  Steps 1–3 are graded against the item's own structure (computed by code, never by an AI). Every
  step works with a finger and with the keyboard (arrow keys move the focus, Enter places). A bar
  is never resized by dragging an edge.
- **Basis.** Drawing mechanics cost working memory (E63, E228) — the tool pays that cost so the
  child pays the thinking cost. Children do draw longer bars for bigger numbers (E64), so "which
  is longer" is a real decision at age 7. The praised digital sequence is build → label → numbers
  → solve, with a self-check (E219, E220). Every model must end in a number sentence (E229, E167
  Rec. 3). Every control grades what it asks; touch and keyboard both (HANDOFF §6).
- **Overturned by.** Evidence that free-canvas drawing on screen transfers better to paper at
  this age (none exists, E179).
- **Note: one bar or two for two-step stories** `[original design]` (R5 C19; ledger E286; added
  2026-11-18). Singapore practice accepts one combined bar or one bar per step. The builder keeps
  grading the **chained** form on model items (steps 1–3 stay code-graded against one schema);
  the self-check shows the one-bar form beside it as another right way (shown, not graded); on
  solve items and on paper a one-bar model is a sound method and earns the marks (DD9). A graded
  one-bar schema waits for evidence that the chained form confuses children.

## DD8 — Paper transfer: one "draw it on paper" item per unit, recorded honestly `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Transfer from a screen model to the paper models the school tests is unstudied (E179);
  manipulatives help retention more than transfer (E164).
- **Default.** Every bar or bond unit from Grade 2 up ends with **one paper item**: "Draw this
  model on paper. Then type the number sentence you wrote under it." The typed sentence is
  graded. The drawing is not seen by the app, so the item is recorded as **"paper practice done"**,
  never as evidence of "knows". The parent page shows the item's text so the parent can glance at
  the paper (a like-instructor move, offered, never assigned — tutoring-app-design §3.12).
- **Basis.** Transfer needs its own practice (E164, E165); the school scores drawn models on
  paper from the Grade 2 Ch 6 test on (E129, E243); a control must never claim to grade what it
  cannot (tutoring-app-design §4).
- **Overturned by.** A camera-based grader that can read a child's drawing reliably (none planned).

## DD9 — Require the model, or accept any sound method: both, as two separate skills `[original design]`

- **Gap.** The ministry accepts any sound working; some classrooms mark the model itself; a child
  was marked down for an equation (G11; E186, E189).
- **Default.** Two kinds of item, two records:
  - **Model items** ("Build the model for this story"): the model is the question. Graded on the
    model (DD7). This is the only evidence for the skill "builds a [type] bar".
  - **Solve items** ("Solve this story"): **any sound method earns the answer mark** — a model, a
    number sentence, a column, a mental route. The app then asks "Can you show it as a model
    too?" as a second, optional step, graded and recorded under the model skill.
  The parent is told this split in one sentence: "At school he may be asked for the model; here
  we practise it on its own and never take away a right answer for skipping it."
- **Basis.** The ministry rule (E186); the praise-then-ask-for-the-other-way practice (E189,
  E184); his misses were about seeing structure, not about getting answers (E241); the production
  deficit means the model must be practised, not merely accepted (E64); the school does test
  completing and drawing bars (E129).
- **Overturned by.** The school's own marking rule, if the owner learns it.

## DD10 — Review and spacing: the module supplies the review the school's book does not `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Daily volume and review spacing are unspecified (G10); Math in Focus gives no continual
  review by design (E104) and short practice sets (E103).
- **Default.**
  - **Returns inside the day:** on a practice day about **a quarter of the items** (2–3 of 10)
    are returns from earlier units, chosen from the weakest skills first (slow, looked-up,
    missed).
  - **Spaced comebacks:** every question missed on a chapter check returns with new numbers at
    about **2, 7 and 21 days** until it is right unaided three times in a row
    (tutoring-app-design §3.13).
  - **Mixed sets by rule:** once a regrouping lesson is taught, its practice mixes items that do
    not need regrouping; once a word-problem type is taught, its practice mixes other types and
    puts the unknown in every position.
  - **No "finished" state:** a unit's skills stay in the return pool until "knows" (METHODOLOGY
    §4) is reached.
  - **No dead end:** a skill served on 6 practice days without one clean day is "stuck"; its
    concrete step re-opens, the model drops one rung and the parent sees "stuck here" with one
    thing to try; the parent may park it after 6 more such days (METHODOLOGY §4 stall rule).
- **Basis.** Gaps compound when books do not reteach (E104, E193); mixing items so the child must
  discriminate is a strong IES recommendation (E90, E167 Rec. 1); the unknown in different places
  is a strong recommendation (E167 Rec. 5); spacing is the tutoring-app-design two-day rule (§2).
- **Overturned by.** Records showing the return share crowds out new learning (new-lesson items
  never reached on practice days).

## DD11 — The chapter check: three sections, feedback held, review after `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Point weights and report categories on the school's tests are known only from the one
  returned paper (G5; E125, E263); the cover sheet's category wording has no public source (E133).
- **Default.** Each unit closes with a **chapter check** in the school's shape: **Section A
  choices**, **Section B short answers** (incl. digits in boxes inside a column, "which way did
  you do it?"), **Section C one worked problem** with a built model or shown steps. About **8
  items**, weighted **2 / 2 / 3** as on his own paper, with at least one third of the parts asking
  for a model or shown steps. **Feedback is held** until the end. Then a **review of misses** walks
  each missed item with the help ladder; the score never changes in review. The parent sees the
  score as counts ("6 of 8 parts"), the items missed with his own answers, and two category rows,
  **"concepts and procedures"** and **"reasoning"**, with the note that these are the module's
  categories, not the school's scale. Every missed item enters the comeback pool (DD10). **No
  check item reuses a lesson example's numbers**, nor has one as its key (L43, L51: an item
  remembered from a lesson is not evidence; CURRICULUM-MAP §7.12). **Once per half-year** a **cumulative check** in the same shape draws from every
  unit taught so far and carries at least one item with a printed bar (the school's Cumulative
  Reviews and Benchmarks, E122; a bar on a Cumulative Review, E129).
- **Basis.** Sections A/B/C are confirmed (E124); his test's points are confirmed (E125); "show
  your work" was 3 of 8 parts (E136); the nearest public category frame is "Concepts and
  Procedures" vs "Communicating Reasoning" (E262); tests hold feedback and then review (tutoring-
  app-design §3.9); a check question read aloud measures maths, not reading (§3.15).
- **Overturned by.** Any further returned school test (points per item, the cover sheet).

## DD12 — Stretch items (sum-and-difference and kin) `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Closed on placement: the two-numbers-from-sum-and-difference type sits in Grade 4 (late
  P4) in the lineage and about two grades above level on his Grade 2 test (E256). Open on
  handling.
- **Default.** Each unit may carry a **stretch lane** of one or two item types the school tests
  above grade. Rules:
  - a stretch type is taught **only after its prerequisite model** (sum-and-difference only after
    comparison bars, DD5 step 7);
  - it is taught as a **thinking-cap-style puzzle** with the heuristic named (draw a diagram;
    check against both conditions) and the help ladder open;
  - it **never gates** a unit's exit and never enters the mastery count for the unit;
  - it is recorded as **"stretch: learning"** until it meets the mastery rule, when it becomes
    **"stretch: knows"**;
  - the parent page says in one line that the school tests it early and where it sits in the
    lineage;
  - the same type returns as **core** in its home grade (Grade 4 for sum-and-difference).
- **Basis.** Above-grade items are common on the school's Grade 2 tests (E128, E26, E27); the
  type is taught by the comparison model (E235); heuristics without a check step fail (E97);
  challenge items sit at chapter end and are optional in many classes (E113, E265); a pass bar is
  not a wall (tutoring-app-design §3.13).
- **Overturned by.** The school dropping above-grade items from its tests (the owner would see
  it on returned papers).

## DD13 — Fluency: this module drills nothing; Kumon-style is the fluency supplier `[original design]`

- **Gap.** Division of labour with the Kumon-style module on fluency (G10, G12).
- **Default.** This module runs **no timed drill and no fact sheets**. It teaches **mental
  strategies** (make ten, compensation, tens-then-ones, jumps on an open number line) as
  **named thinking routes**, practised untimed, 3–4 items at a time, with "which way did you do
  it?" on a sample. Speed is **recorded** per item (monotonic clock) for the mastery rule and the
  parent, and **never shown** to the child. When a child's column fluency is weak, the parent page
  names the Kumon-style sheet range that covers it; this module does not add sheets.
- **Basis.** Timed fluency is a strong recommendation and is already supplied in volume by the
  Kumon-style module (E167 Rec. 6, E106, E108); the usual family split is drill outside, concepts
  in school (E195); mental strategies are a different skill from column speed (E80–E84), and the
  school's "add mentally" item was a hidden miss for a child whose columns are fast (E236);
  record speed, do not show it (tutoring-app-design §3.5).
- **Overturned by.** The Kumon-style module being dropped from his day (then DD13 is re-opened).
- **Cross-reference (2026-11-18; R5 C14, C6; ledger E277).** Blocks show the structure of a
  column; known facts give its answers. A child who misses the facts-that-cross-ten pair (DD4
  warm block; G2-U02-EC) is routed to fact supply here, not to an extra regrouping lesson, and
  the regrouping lessons fade the chart once two trades are right (GRADE-2 U02 L3).

## DD14 — Division of labour: one method per skill, the link said out loud `[original design]`

- **Gap.** No public account of a child doing three programmes at once (E218, E180).
- **Default.** METHOD-LEDGER.md is binding: for every skill more than one module touches, this
  module teaches **its own one method**, and the teacher **says the link** to the sister method
  in one sentence the first time the skill appears and whenever the child uses the sister
  method. The module never introduces a third, unconnected method. The word **"number bond" means
  the part-whole diagram** in this module; memorised sums are called **"facts"**. The parent is
  never asked to teach a second method.
- **Basis.** The real clashes are the meaning of the carry, the "add mentally" items and word
  problems, not column vs no column (E201–E210); "number bond" has two meanings (E200);
  parents do not recognise the representations (E182); the tutoring-app-design parent law
  (§3.12).
- **Overturned by.** Nothing; this is a product rule from HANDOFF §8.

## DD15 — Heuristics: named moves practised inside problems, one per unit, with a check step `[original design]`

- **Gap.** The ministry's and Math in Focus's heuristic-by-grade sequences are unpublished (G6,
  E266); only PM 2022's per-chapter list is confirmed (E264).
- **Default.** Each unit names **one heuristic** and practises it inside that unit's problems,
  ending in a **thinking-cap-style puzzle** at unit end (optional for exit, DD12). The grade
  sequence follows E268: Grade 1 make a list, guess and check, look for a pattern; Grade 2 adds
  work backwards and draw a diagram; Grade 3 supposition and act it out; Grade 4 before-after,
  tabulating, simplify; Grade 5 restate, write an equation. Every heuristic is paired with a
  **check step** ("Does my answer fit every clue?"). Heuristics are never a separate taught
  skill with its own mastery count. In Grades 1–2 the check step is the last step of DD17's
  story routine ("check the answer in the story"; 2026-11-18, R5 C18, C20).
- **Basis.** E268 is the current lineage reference; the four-step frame
  (understand → plan → carry out → check) is the school's poster (E92); heuristics without
  monitoring fail (E97); no controlled study shows general heuristic transfer in Grades 1–3
  (E178), so they are practised as moves, not taught as a subject.
- **Overturned by.** A published Math in Focus per-chapter heuristic list.

## DD16 — The written "why": a reason frame, graded by choice first, typed later `[original design]`

- **Gap.** What earns the point on a written explanation is teacher-only (G9; E126, E261); he lost
  his Q6 point on the reason (E237).
- **Default.** Explain items use a **reason frame**: "[what is wrong / what I did] **because**
  [place-value reason]." By grade:
  - **Grades 1–2:** the child **taps the true claims** from one-row-each statements (graded, e.g.
    "the 6 of 360 stands for 6 tens" / "the 6 of 360 stands for 6 ones"), then **says or types**
    the sentence (formative only, read aloud back to him).
  - **Grade 3 up:** the child **types** the frame; a rubric built from the item's own content
    (the expected place-value words) gives a formative reading; the point for the reason is
    earned by the **tap step**, which stays on every explain item.
  On an **error-analysis** item the order is fixed: the child writes the fix and commits it first;
  only then do the claim rows appear, then the sentence — so a row that states the repair can
  never be read before the fix is made (L25, L33; METHODOLOGY §2 Step 7). Rows that apply the
  idea to the item ("45 + 8 is already more than 52") are preferred to rows that recite the
  teacher's rule (tutoring-app-design §3.8).
  The reading never moves a score (tutoring-app-design §7). The parent sees the sentence and its
  reading. The module keeps a short list of **reasoning words** per unit ("regroup", "a ten is ten
  ones", "the digit's place") and expects them.
- **Basis.** Mathematical language is a strong recommendation (E102, E167 Rec. 2); prompted
  self-explanation helps only when scaffolded and can hurt or crowd out practice (E174); the
  place-value "why" is the concept behind carry and borrow (E75); explain items recur from Grade
  1 (E258, E259); one claim per row, graded (tutoring-app-design §4).
- **Overturned by.** The school's rubric, if the owner sees one.

## DD17 — Reading load: read aloud by default through Grade 2, short sentences, no key words `[original design]`

- **Gap.** The reading load of Grade 1–2 word problems in this approach is unquantified (G13).
- **Default.** Every word problem is **read aloud by default in Grades 1–2** and on tap from Grade
  3. Grade 1–2 problems use **at most 10 words per sentence, two sentences of story, one
  question**, and the picture is the kind of thing the words name. **No key-word teaching**
  ("altogether means add") anywhere. Each unit lists the **story words** it uses ("the rest",
  "fewer", "how many more") and the teacher says each once with a picture. A miss on a long
  problem where the child's answer shows he did not parse the task is flagged **reading**, not
  maths, and the parent is told so.
  **Story routine, Grades 1–2** `[original design]` (2026-11-18; R5 C18, C20; ledger E288,
  E289): every story item runs one fixed short sequence — (1) the story is read aloud; (2) the
  child says it back in his own words (at Grades 1–2 a tap on who and what the story is about,
  formative); (3) he taps what is asked; (4) he labels the model (bond or bar); (5) he checks the
  answer in the story. **Key words are never taught as rules.** If the child offers one ("more
  means add"), it is treated as a clue the model must confirm: the teacher says "check it on the
  bar", and the model decides. The step names are the module's own and stay open
  (Speculative) until the names of the Singapore P1–P2 five-step routine are found in a readable
  source (R5 C20: the steps sit in an image that could not be read).
- **Basis.** The ministry checks word counts and language demand for young learners (E191);
  read-aloud is valued in Grade 1–2 apps (E225); a Singapore P2 class names word meanings as a
  typical difficulty (E248); key words undo the method (E211, E167, E52); measure the maths, not
  the reading (tutoring-app-design §3.15); band screen laws (§4).
- **Overturned by.** Records showing read-aloud on by default slows a reading child (he can turn
  it off from Grade 2 by his own streak).

## DD24 — Every learner: ask the parent, start where the child was, mix in what comes next `[original design]` (owner direction, DECISIONS M11)

- **Parent intake (about two minutes, before the first day).** Grade · the school's maths book if
  known · the chapter the class is on now · the topics the child finds hard (tick boxes per unit,
  in plain words) · anything the parent wants added ("more advanced work", "word problems") ·
  questions per day (O3). Photos of school work are never asked for (DECISIONS M6, M9).
- **Start where the child was.** Placement = a **short review (two days at most) of the last
  chapter the class finished**, in the school's formats — a **mastery** check, "did he learn what
  was taught?" — then the entry check of the unit beside the class's current chapter (DD3), a
  **readiness** check, "is he ready to begin this?" (the two placement models of the free
  Singapore-lineage tests, R5 C1; ledger E271). Units the parent ticked as hard come first as repair units
  (HANDOFF §8, schedule from weakness).
- **The daily mix.** On a practice day of N questions: about **60 %** from the current unit, about
  **25 %** returns from earlier units, weakest first (DD10), and **one or two preview items** from
  the next unit or from the advanced topic the parent asked for. Preview items are labelled "try a
  new one", have the full help ladder, and never count against the child. Two right first tries on
  separate days mark that skill "already knows", so its later lessons can be shortened.
  **Default preview in Grade 2 autumn** (2026-11-18; R5 C6, C26; ledger E284, E285): while the
  child is in G2-U02 to G2-U04, the preview is **reading** a part-whole or comparison bar with
  numbers within 20 (which sentence does it say? which bar fits this story?), never solving, so
  bars are familiar before G2-U05 starts them at 3-digit size.
- **Parent page per unit: a four-part home note** `[original design]` (2026-11-18; R5 C6 for the
  shape only; ledger E290). (1) What the unit teaches; (2) the unit's words, each with one picture
  (DD20); (3) "this may look different from how you learned it" (e.g. regrouping is what you may
  call carrying or borrowing; two subtraction routes); (4) "at home": two or three short games or
  questions, offered, never assigned (TAD §3.12), with "ask him to explain it in the unit's
  words"; fact practice suggested as a sort into known / slow / not yet, never timed by the
  module (DD13). All text is the module's own.
- **Basis.** Interleaving and spaced returns (DD10); "knows" needs two separate days
  (tutoring-app-design §2); the parent is a like-instructor who offers, never assigns (§3.12).
- **Overturned by.** Records showing preview items lowering first-try accuracy on the current
  unit, or parents finding the intake too long.

## DD25 — Start from the beginning; a placement test decides where each child really starts `[original design]` (owner direction, DECISIONS M18; refines DD3, DD4, DD24)

- **Default.** A new learner's path starts at **Unit 1 of their grade**. Nothing is skipped on a
  guess.
- **The placement test** (before the first lesson; read aloud through Grade 2; untimed; touch
  pad and keyboard; feedback held to the end, as in a chapter check):
  - built like the free publisher placement tests (R5 C1–C4; P3.1 rows): short, **a ladder**
    through the grade's units in order, with the previous grade's prerequisites first;
  - **two items of the same structure per unit** (DD3) on that unit's core skill, in the school's
    formats; about 15–25 items for a grade;
  - **stops early** after misses on two units in a row in the number / operation / bar-model
    strand, so a child is never walked through a long run of things not yet taught.
- **Reading it.** Per unit: both items right on the first try → **placed out** ("already
  knows"); one right → **start with a short check** (its entry check, DD3); none → **start here**.
  The start point is the **first unit that is not placed out**. The parent sees the result per
  unit, in counts, and can move the start earlier or later.
- **Placed-out is confirmed, not assumed.** A placed-out unit's skills come back as returns in
  the first weeks (DD10). Right unaided on a second separate day → "knows" (tutoring-app-design
  §2). A miss → that lesson opens and the skill becomes "learning".
- **Re-check.** The parent can re-run placement at any time (the contract's `recheckSupported`).
- **The first learner.** He takes the Grade 2 placement test, which also uses his school test
  reading and the regrouping probe (DD4) as prior evidence. His start comes from the result, not
  from a choice made in advance. The December test prep is a separate add-on (TEST-PREP-SPEC).
- **Basis.** Free placement tests of Singapore-lineage publishers (P3.1); "knows" on two separate
  days (TAD §2); schedule from weakness (HANDOFF §8).
- **Overturned by.** Records showing placed-out skills failing their confirm returns often, which
  would mean the placement items are too easy or too few.
