## §1 Grade goals

**Common Core Grade 5** (E5): the place-value system to thousandths and powers of ten, multi-digit
multiplication, division with 2-digit divisors, the four operations with decimals to hundredths
(5.NBT); numerical expressions with brackets, patterns and relationships (5.OA); fraction ± with
unlike denominators, fraction × and limited ÷, fraction as division (5.NF); conversions within a
system, line plots, volume (5.MD); the coordinate plane and classification of 2-D figures (5.G).

**Where the Singapore order runs earlier or later** (E13, E14, E34): earlier — percentage and rate
(P5; Grade 6, E26), angle facts and the triangle angle sum (P5; not in Common Core Grade 5, E5),
area of a triangle (P5; not in Common Core Grade 5, E5), decimals × ÷ by a whole (P4; Grade 5); later — the coordinate plane (secondary; Grade 5),
general fraction ÷ (P6 = Grade 6, the same), ratio (P6; Grade 6, the same). The school's book carries
Ch 9 ratio and Ch 10 percent, which are Grade 6 standards (E26, E269); the module keeps them as
school-beside units so the child is not alone in class, builds no new bar for them (DD23), and the
parent page says Common Core places them in Grade 6.

**The year's model work.** Disks carry × and ÷ by 10, 100, 1,000 across the point (E269) and decimal
× ÷; the area model carries 3-digit × 2-digit and fraction × fraction; equal-unit bars carry ÷ by a
unit fraction; the hundred square carries decimal × and percent; unit cubes in layers carry volume
(E120). Bars follow the lineage sequence for Grade 5 — fraction and decimal word problems — and
**every unit bar carries a letter**, so the model and the first equation are one picture (DD23; E65,
E250, E251). Heuristics new this grade: restate the problem, write an equation (E268).

**Formats and explaining.** Chapter checks add multiple select and the shown-work constructed
response (E269). The reason frame is typed; the tap step earns the point (DD16).

## §4 Two expanded units

### §4.1 G5-U02 Fractions and mixed numbers: ± unlike denominators

**Teacher explanation covers** (short, elicit-not-narrate, L25): pieces can be counted together only
when they are the **same size**, so adding halves and thirds starts with a question — "what piece fits
both?" — and the answer is a piece whose count is a common multiple of the two bottoms; cutting both
bars into that piece changes nothing but the name (the amount stays still); the smallest common piece
is tidy, any common piece works; a mixed number with too small a fraction part opens one whole into
pieces, the same move as opening a ten; before the exact answer, a benchmark check — more than 1 or
less? — so a wrong answer feels wrong; a fraction is also a **division** — 3 wholes shared among 4 give
each 3/4; and the letter: a bar whose unit is unknown gets a **letter on the unit**, and the bar is
then an equation (3u = 84) — "the letter stands for this box; your bar is the equation drawn"
(METHOD-LEDGER C7; DD23; E65). The links once: "finding a common denominator on your Kumon sheet is
cutting both bars into the same-size pieces" (E3); "Ms. Wren's 'why can't we add tops and bottoms?'
is this picture" (E3).

**Guided progression:**
1. *Concrete.* Two paper strips, one in halves and one in thirds (L1; N-I12 on screen). The child
   tries to add a half and a third and meets the question "what do I call this?"
2. *Pictorial, the child builds.* Both strips cut into sixths (N-I12); the amounts stay still, the
   names change; the sum read off. Then a mixed-number subtraction with a whole opened. Then three
   bars shared among four for fraction as division.
3. *Abstract.* The common multiple found from a table (G4-U01 L5), the renamed fractions written,
   the sum or difference; the benchmark check typed first ("> 1").
4. *The bar with a letter.* A fraction story; the builder asks the unit count from the fraction's
   bottom, places the known amount over its units, writes **u** on one unit (a letter chip, §7), and
   the child writes 3u = 84 → u = 28 (N-I8). The item's model is never visible before commit (L33).
5. *Guided practice* of 3–4 items with the help ladder: what it means (re-read with "same pieces"
   highlighted) → use what you know (empty bars appear) → a way to finish (both bars are cut; he
   adds) → the answer with the model filled. No rung below 4 states the answer.
6. *Explain* (one per lesson day): tap the true claims, then type the sentence (N-I10): "2/3 + 3/4

### §4.2 G5-U05 Four operations of decimals

**Teacher explanation covers:** a decimal is whole numbers continued — ten of a disk make one of the
next, in both directions, and the point only marks where the ones column is; so adding decimals is
lining up **columns**, which is lining up the points (METHOD-LEDGER E6: "lining up the points on your
Kumon sheet is lining up the disk columns"); subtracting across zeros opens a one into ten tenths and a
tenth into ten hundredths, the same move as opening a hundred; multiplying by a whole is groups of
disks, and the point is placed by **what the disks are** (372 hundredths is 3.72), with an estimate to
confirm (E7: "counting the decimal places is a shortcut for what the disks show"); a decimal times a
decimal is the overlap on the hundred square, and tenths of tenths are hundredths; dividing by a
decimal is **restated** — shift both numbers the same way until the divisor is whole, because the
question "how many 0.5s in 4.5?" has the same answer as "how many 5s in 45?"; and a decimal story
gets the same bar as any story, with a letter on the unit when the unit is unknown (DD23).

**Guided progression:**
1. *Concrete.* Disks on a chart with 1, 0.1, 0.01 columns (N-I3). The teacher asks "what do we have
   too many of?" at one trade; the child trades ten hundredths for a tenth and ten tenths for a one.
2. *Pictorial, the child builds.* The pre-trade chart (N-F2 pre-trade only); the hundred square cut
   by the child into 0.3 rows and 0.4 columns (hundred-square input); a disk chart shared into
   groups for ÷.
3. *Abstract.* The column with the points aligned (N-I1 decimal form); the product's point placed
   after naming the unit ("372 hundredths"); the division restated with a shift of both numbers.
4. *The bar with a letter.* A money or measurement story; the builder asks the unit count, the known
   parts and where the letter goes; the equation written (4n + 2.60 = 13.40) and solved in two
   steps.
5. *Guided practice* with the help ladder as §4.1.
6. *Explain:* "The point in 3.72 goes after the 3 because 372 hundredths is 3 wholes and 72
   hundredths."

**A practice day's anatomy** (N = 10): seven new items — one sum with unequal decimal lengths, one
difference across zeros, one decimal × whole with the estimate typed first, one decimal × decimal on
the square, one ÷ by a whole with disks, one ÷ by a decimal restated, one bar story with a letter.
Three returns (e.g. U04 L4 rounding a decimal, U02 L2 an unlike sum, U01 L5 an expression with
brackets). Pictures: disks in the pre-trade state only; the hundred square unshaded until the child
cuts it; the estimate recorded before the exact is typed.

## §6 Entry and exit profile

**Entry (what a child needs to begin Grade 5 units — equals the Grade 4 exit in GRADE-4.md §6).**
Builds numbers to 1,000,000 with disks, rounds to any place and says a digit is ten times the one to
its right; finds factor pairs and multiples, tells prime from composite, continues a pattern from a
rule; multiplies 2-digit × 2-digit through the four-box area model and the column, multiplies 4-digit
× 1-digit, divides 3- and 4-digit numbers by a 1-digit number with disks then the written method,
estimates first, checks with × and +, and says what a remainder means; **builds** multiplicative-
comparison, before-after and sum-and-difference bars and checks both clues; makes equivalent
fractions and simplest form, compares with like pieces, shows mixed and improper fractions, adds and
subtracts like denominators across a whole, multiplies a fraction by a whole and finds a fraction of a
set with a bar; shows tenths and hundredths with disks and the hundred square, compares decimals,
writes fourths and halves as decimals; converts larger to smaller units with a table and a bar; uses
the area and perimeter formulas, finds a missing side and a composite area; measures and draws angles
and adds angle parts on a bar; classifies triangles and quadrilaterals with a typed reason and draws
lines of symmetry; builds tables and line plots with fractions and reads a line graph. Checked by each
unit's entry check (DD3).

**Exit (what "knows" looks like at the end of Grade 5, per skill, two days, first try, no scaffold;
METHODOLOGY §4).** Shifts disks for × and ÷ by 10, 100, 1,000 across the point and reads powers of
ten; multiplies 3-digit × 2-digit and divides by a 2-digit divisor with an estimate first and a check;
orders the steps of an expression with brackets and writes an expression from words; adds and
subtracts fractions with unlike denominators by cutting to like pieces, including mixed numbers with
a whole opened, with a benchmark check; sees a fraction as a division; multiplies fraction × fraction
on the area model and mixed numbers with four boxes, says whether a product is bigger or smaller
without computing, divides a whole by a unit fraction and a unit fraction by a whole with bars; shows,
compares and rounds decimals to thousandths; adds, subtracts, multiplies and divides decimals with
the point placed by place value; finds volume by layers and by formula, a missing dimension and a
composite solid; makes line plots with fractions, plots and reads points, and pairs terms from two
rules; classifies polygons in a hierarchy with a typed reason and checks a triangle's angle sum;
**builds a bar with a letter on the unit** for fraction, decimal, ratio and percent stories and writes
and solves its equation. Stretch state shown separately; school-beside units (ratio, percent) are
flagged Grade 6 on the parent page.
