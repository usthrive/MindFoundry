## G1 — The school's own Grade 2 calendar and current chapter (OWNER item)

**Why it matters.** The Grade 2 entry plan must land part-whole and comparison bars before the
class meets them in Ch 4, and rehearse the Ch 3 subtraction formats while they are being tested.
Book B order varies by district (Time and Money Feb–May; Mass moved or dropped; tables sometimes
skipped), so after Ch 5 the plan cannot be timed from public data. Rows: E37, E38, E137–E141.

**Path.** Not a web search: the no-personal-data rule forbids searching the school or district by
name. **→ design default until the owner supplies it:** assume the median of three public district
calendars (E139): Ch 3 Subtraction now (mid-Nov), Ch 4 bar models late Nov / early Dec into
January, Ch 5 Length next; treat Book B as unordered and key each Grade 2 unit to its own chapter
so the order can be swapped later. **Owner can settle it** in minutes with any one of: the
teacher's pacing note or newsletter; the School-to-Home letter at the front of the current Extra
Practice chapter (it names the chapter); the titles of assignments on the child's Ed login; the
date of the next chapter test.

## G7 — Which regrouping rule(s) he holds now → design default

**Why it matters.** The Grade 2 entry plan should start at the rule he actually holds. Q2 has two
rules that fit (E232); Q3/Q5b share "each column alone" (E233, E234); one test is ±1 part (E231).

**→ Design default: a short placement probe before the first lesson.** Two or more items per
structure, original numbers: ones that sum to 13, 23 and 31 across 2, 3 and 4 addends (separates
"carry is always 1" from a mis-added ones column); missing addend with and without regrouping;
digits in boxes in a column with and without carries; one "add mentally" item with a "which way did
you do it?" follow-up. Label a rule only when two same-structure items agree; re-probe after a few
days, because bugs migrate. **Basis:** bugs are systematic, arise as repairs, and migrate, so one
answer cannot fix a rule (E77); mix problem types so habits are challenged (E90).

## G8 — Building a bar model on screen at age 7, and transfer to paper → design default

**Why it matters.** It is the core interaction of the module and the school scores paper: complete
a blank bar (Ch 4 test), draw one (Ch 6 test). No study compares screen and paper at Grades 1–3
(E179, E227), so a search will not settle it. Rows: E64, E135, E166, E212, E215, E219, E224, E228.

**→ Design default: guided construction that fades, ending in a number sentence, with periodic paper
transfer.** Steps: read (read-aloud in Grades 1–2) → build bars → label known parts and the "?" →
put numbers → write the number sentence → solve → check against every condition. Fade the scaffold
over a unit: supplied model → label it → complete it → choose and size the bars himself. A
self-check compares his model with a reference model. Every few sessions, one item is drawn on
paper (parent confirms it was done), because recognising a diagram is not drawing one. **Basis:**
fading from supplied to child-built (E59); production deficit (E64); guided construction is the
praised pattern, fill-the-blank is criticised (E219, E224); representations must connect explicitly
to notation (IES Rec. 3, strong, E167; E229); drawing mechanics cost working memory (E63, E228);
virtual manipulatives show a moderate effect (E166).

## G9 — What earns the point on a written "why" → design default

**Why it matters.** He lost Q6's point on the "why" (E237); neither existing module grades it; Math
in Focus rubrics exist but sit in the teacher-only Assessment Guide (E126). Rows: E101, E126, E174,
E210, E237.

**→ Design default: a two-part check — (1) the right answer or correction, (2) a reason in
place-value language that names the unit ("the 4 is 4 tens, so it goes under the tens").** Start
with a structured "because" frame and fade it to free text; keep prompts few so they do not crowd
out practice. **Basis:** IES Rec. 2 mathematical language (strong, E102, E167); prompted
self-explanation helps only when scaffolded and can hurt (E174); Ma's place-value "why" (E75).

## G10 — Daily practice volume and review spacing → design default

**Why it matters.** Math in Focus practice sets are short and give no cumulative review (E103,
E104, E193); Kumon already gives a large daily dose of columns (E106, E108); no item-per-day counts
exist for Singapore-style books (E107). Rows: E103, E104, E107, E108, E193.

**→ Design default: short daily mixed sets plus spaced cumulative review; no duplicated column
drill.** Each set mixes regrouping and non-regrouping items, unknowns in different positions, and
2-, 3- and 4-addend sums; earlier units return in a weekly cumulative review. **Basis:** IES Rec. 1
mix problem types so students must discriminate (strong, E90); IES Rec. 5 unknown in every position
(strong, E167); Singapore books do not reteach, so gaps compound (E104); Kumon covers timed fluency
(IES Rec. 6, E167; E106).

## G11 — Require the model, or accept any sound method? → design default

**Why it matters.** He has a fast column habit (E201); children with a fast method resist bars
(E189); the ministry accepts any sound working (E186), but his school's Grade 2 tests ask him to
complete and draw bar models (E129). Rows: E186, E189, E190, E206.

**→ Design default: never mark a correct answer wrong for its method; on items whose purpose is
the model, also ask for the model ("now show it with bars").** The parent report says plainly that
the school's tests may ask for the model. **Basis:** ministry rule (E186); the "both ways" teacher
practice (E189); Math in Focus tests (E129); seeing structure is what his misses lacked (E241).

## G12 — Division of labour with Kumon and Best Brains-style → design default

**Why it matters.** Nothing public documents his three-programme situation (E218) or the effect of
combining programmes (E180). Rows: E180, E195, E203, E218.

**→ Design default: Kumon owns fact and column fluency; Best Brains-style keeps its weekly concept
breadth; school-math owns place-value meaning (the trade), part-whole and bar models, mental
strategies, word problems by structure, and explain.** No timed column drill in school-math; a
small vocabulary map ("number bond picture" vs "fact"; "regroup/rename" vs "carry/borrow").
**Basis:** the common family split (E195); timed fluency is strong evidence and already covered
(E167, E106); the clash table (E201–E210); the "number bond" double meaning (E200).

## G13 — Reading load of Grade 1–2 word problems → design default

**Why it matters.** Q7 needed holding two conditions from text; reading load is a known barrier at
this age (E191). Rows: E191, E192, E225.

**→ Design default: read-aloud on by default in Grades 1–2 (tap to hear again), short sentences, no
key-word cues; when a word problem is missed, offer the same problem read aloud to separate a
reading miss from a maths miss.** **Basis:** the ministry checks word counts and language demand for
young learners (E191); read-aloud valued in Grade 1–2 apps (E225); avoid key words (E167, E211).
