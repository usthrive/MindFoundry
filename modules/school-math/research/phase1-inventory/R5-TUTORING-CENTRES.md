# R5 — Tutoring centres, programmes, publisher freebies and free tools (school-math)

Written 2026-11-18 06:49 EST (from `date`) by research lane R5. No child's name or personal data
anywhere; no sign-ups; no personal data in any search. Web pages were read as untrusted data.

**Scope.** Centres and programmes that teach Singapore-style maths (US and Singapore), the free
material they publish, free teacher material from Singapore-lineage publishers and educators,
government mastery material that descends from the Singapore model (England), and free on-screen
manipulatives. **What R1–R4 already hold is not repeated** (INVENTORY.md §1–§4: the ministry
syllabus, Math in Focus structure, EdReports, the IES guide, Thinking Blocks, Toy Theater, KooBits'
syllabus and enrichment pages, the Schoolbag Q&A, Eureka parent tips). Where R5 touches one of
those, it says so and adds only what is new.

**Rule kept.** Free to view is not free to copy. This file records structure, sequence, methods,
mistakes and terms — never items, pages, layouts, characters or screens. Quotes are ≤ 15 words.
Placement-test PDFs were opened only to count and classify their questions; no item is reproduced
here or anywhere in the module.

**Labels.** Confirmed = read on the source itself. Inferred = read through a search excerpt, a
secondary page, or reasoned from a confirmed fact. Speculative = a lead not checked. Strength
High / Med / Low as in the other lanes. Citation style for later phases: `R5:C12`.

## 2. Findings by topic

### 2.1 Placement — how Singapore-lineage programmes place a new child

- **F1. Free Grade 1–2 placement tests exist.** Singapore Math Inc. publishes free placement
  tests for every half-grade from kindergarten (Dimensions KA) and from 1A (Primary Mathematics)
  — including 1A, 1B, 2A, 2B. This **corrects** INVENTORY §3 item 9 and gap G19 ("no Grade 1–2
  placement test was found"): none exists for Math in Focus, but the sister programmes have them.
  (C1, C2–C5 — Confirmed · High)
- **F2. Two placement models, side by side.** Dimensions and Primary Mathematics US/Standards
  tests ask "has the child **mastered** the level just finished?" — pass 2A, take 2B; fail 2B,
  start there. Primary Mathematics 2022 tests ask "is the child **ready to begin** this level?" —
  not ready for 2B, start at 2A. (C1 — Confirmed · High)
- **F3. Not timed, not scored — watched.** The publisher says the tests should not be timed
  (allow at least an hour) and should not be scored; the adult watches how the child solves,
  which errors appear and which ideas are hard. The tests sample "representative skills", not
  every topic. (C1 — Confirmed · High) A reseller-tutor (C9) still reads them as pass/fail
  ("misses only one problem → ready") — practice drifts toward scoring. (C9 — Confirmed · Med)
- **F4. Size and shape.** Dimensions 1A: 20 numbered questions over 8 pages plus a key; 2A: about
  23 questions over ~11 pages, many with lettered parts; 2B: ~10 pages. Primary Mathematics 2022
  "for 2A": 20 questions with a mark count printed beside each (e.g. [3], [5]). So one half-grade
  test is ~20–23 questions and ~40–60 answer blanks. (C2–C5 — Confirmed · High, counted)
- **F5. What a Grade 2 test probes (by kind, no items).** Dimensions 2A: numbers to 1,000 in
  digits, words and units; patterns; order and compare (incl. a number given as units in mixed
  order); **reading a supplied bar model to complete an equation**; written + and − with
  renaming; **two-part word problems where part (a)'s answer feeds part (b)** (a part-whole total
  then a comparison); length, weight; × and ÷ meaning with arrays. Dimensions 2B opens with
  **mental addition and subtraction** and a missing-number question, then money and fractions.
  (C3, C4 — Confirmed · High)
- **F6. Difficulty ladders inside one question.** In the Primary Mathematics 2022 readiness test
  for 2A, each computation question runs from no-renaming to renaming in its lettered parts (e.g.
  a 1-digit add-on, a tens add-on, then a 2-digit sum with a trade), so the adult sees **where**
  the child breaks, not just whether. It also mixes "use a related addition fact" and "complete
  the fact family" with two one-step stories. (C5 — Confirmed · High)
- **F7. Placement advice around the tests.** Children from another curriculum may start a level
  lower; small gaps are filled by the teacher or with earlier-level pages, large gaps by going back
  a level (C1). The online programme lets families switch level up to four times a year and warns
  that switching curricula is a common cause of gaps (C9). (Confirmed · High / Med)
- **F8. Centres place with a diagnostic, not a public test.** Every centre seen (C11) offers a
  free "assessment" or "trial lesson" as its intake; none publishes the instrument. (Inferred · Low
  — absence is the finding)

### 2.2 Grade 2 place value to 1,000

- **F9. Dimensions Grade 2 Ch 1 uses place-value discs from the start** (not only blocks), with
  "10 tens make 100", a number as hundreds-tens-ones, and its expanded sum; comparison by the
  greatest place first, with < and >. Math in Focus waits for discs until Grade 4 (INVENTORY #11),
  so the two Singapore lines differ on the object. (C6 letter #1 — Confirmed · High)
- **F10. Non-standard partitioning is a named Year 3 (≈ US Grade 2) goal.** England's guidance
  asks children to compose and decompose 3-digit numbers in standard **and** non-standard ways
  (e.g. a number split into two multiples of ten, or into a hundreds-and-tens part and a ones
  part), to meet units **in varied order** so that "40 + 300 + 2" is read as 342, and to subtract
  any one place-value part from the whole. (C14 3NPV–2 — Confirmed · High) Oak teaches it as its
  own lesson and names the misconception: children struggle to **partition hundreds and tens
  together**; the response is to rearrange base-ten blocks while partitioning. (C12 — Confirmed ·
  High)
- **F11. Unitising: "how many tens in 340?".** The guidance's first Year 3 criterion is that 10
  tens make a hundred **and** the child can say how many tens are in any 3-digit multiple of ten;
  facts are scaled by ten and a hundred (8 + 7 = 15, so 80 + 70 = 150, 800 + 700 = 1,500).
  (C14 3NPV–1, 3NF–3 — Confirmed · High) A Singapore tuition page uses the same move in P2
  renaming (a number read as so many tens). (C19 — Confirmed · Med)
- **F12. Place value on a number line is part of the place-value unit.** Oak's 15-lesson
  3-digit unit places numbers on marked lines (lesson 3), estimates on unmarked lines (lesson 4,
  anchors at the hundreds, then halfway marks), and names the misconception: children cannot
  carry 0–100 positions to 0–1,000 — the response is to set 19 beside 190 and 37 beside 370. The
  guidance adds "previous and next multiple of 10 and 100" and reading scales split into 2, 4, 5
  or 10 parts. (C12, C14 3NPV–3, 3NPV–4 — Confirmed · High)
- **F13. Oak's 3-digit unit order:** represent several ways → count on/back → marked line →
  unmarked line → compare/order → ± multiples of 100 → tens as a multiplication of ten → partition
  in different ways → ± to and from multiples of 100 → bridging 100 → problems → count in 2, 20,
  5, 50, 25. (C12 — Confirmed · High)

### 2.3 Regrouping in addition and subtraction

- **F14. Order: master column addition with regrouping before column subtraction.** No-regroup
  first (layout, right to left), then regroup ones, then tens, then both; subtraction after.
  (C14 3AS–2; C12 column-addition lessons 1–10 and column-subtraction 1–5 — Confirmed · High) This
  matches our G2-U02 → G2-U04 order.
- **F15. Blocks only at first, for structure — not to find answers.** Base-ten apparatus is used
  "only initially" to show the algorithm's structure; once understood, each column is done from
  **known facts** within 20 (the guidance makes facts that bridge ten a named prerequisite).
  Dimensions tells parents the same: children who know 7 + 8 = 15 from memory recall the column
  steps more easily. (C14 3AS–2, 3NF–1; C6 letter #3 — Confirmed · High)
- **F16. Unitising language for every column.** The guidance's language focus is "3 ones plus 5
  ones is equal to 8 ones" and "4 tens plus 2 tens is equal to 6 tens"; Dimensions writes "7 ones
  + 5 ones = 12 ones; 12 ones = 1 ten 2 ones" and, in subtraction, "4 tens 4 ones = 3 tens 14
  ones". Dr Yeap's parent video frames the root idea: only things with the same noun can be added.
  Oak's misconception list has the mirror error: a child says 3 + 6 for 30 + 60. (C14, C6 #3, C16,
  C12 — Confirmed · High / Med)
- **F17. Oak's named regrouping misconceptions:** regrouping incorrectly, not writing the
  regrouped ten, or not adding it into the tens total; response: stress that the regrouped amount
  is **a ten** and return to blocks. Zero marks an empty place in layout. Children over-invest in
  precise estimates — estimates should be quick. (C12 — Confirmed · High via lesson pages)
- **F18. Three or more addends: add digits in the most efficient order** inside a column (look
  for tens first). (C14 3AS–2 — Confirmed · High) This bears directly on the four-addend miss.
- **F19. The column is not always the best route.** Complements to 100 (3AS–1), unitising in
  tens (12 tens + 13 tens), and counting on when the two numbers are close are taught as choices;
  Oak's "knowns and unknowns" lesson says to count on from the smaller number when numbers are
  close. Children check by the inverse operation. (C14 3AS–1, 3AS–2; C12 — Confirmed · High)
- **F20. Subtraction layout: "write the whole first".** Dimensions tells parents the vertical
  subtraction always puts the whole on top, and pictures the same story as a part-whole bar
  beside it. Its language says "not enough ones … regroup one ten as 10 ones". Parents are told
  adults may know regrouping as "carrying" or "borrowing", and three colours of poker chips can
  stand in for discs at home. (C6 #3 — Confirmed · High)
- **F21. A Singapore P2 page teaches renaming by "cannot subtract 8 tens from 2 tens"**, then
  rewrites one hundred and two tens as twelve tens; checks by adding back. (C19 — Confirmed · Med)

### 2.4 Missing numbers and the part-whole relationship

- **F22. With 3-digit numbers, missing-number items must be rearranged — and it is not
  automatic.** The guidance: in Years 1–2 children solved missing numbers from known bonds or by
  counting on; with 3-digit numbers they must rewrite the equation, and "teachers should not
  assume" this transfers by itself. It names **three** types — missing addend, **missing
  subtrahend**, **missing minuend** — and a sort step first: is a part or the whole missing?
  Language focus: a missing part → subtract the other part from the whole; a missing whole → add
  the parts. (C14 3AS–3 — Confirmed · High) This is the Q3 miss's mechanism in an official text.
- **F23. Write all eight equations a diagram shows.** Children practise writing the full set of
  eight addition and subtraction equations for one partitioning diagram or bar, and learn the
  names addend, sum, minuend, subtrahend, difference. (C14 3AS–3 — Confirmed · High) Oak's unit
  does the same in lessons 1–3 (rearrange addition, then subtraction, then relate them) before
  "knowns and unknowns" (4–5) and "rearrange and solve" (6); its misconception: children mix up
  the parts and the whole when a number is missing — response: sketch a bar often; the minuend
  and the sum are both a whole. (C12 — Confirmed · High)
- **F24. Oak places the additive-relationship unit before column addition** (unit 9, then
  column addition unit 10), and mental 3-digit strategies before both (unit 8). (C12 — Confirmed ·
  High) Our G2-U03 sits after U02; both orders are defensible; the finding supports teaching the
  part/whole sort early.

### 2.5 Bar models: part-whole and comparison

- **F25. Dimensions introduces bars in Grade 2 Ch 2 with numbers within 20** — both part-whole
  and comparison at once — "to focus on the visual understanding", and only in Ch 3 do children
  read bars with 3-digit numbers. (C6 letter #2 — Confirmed · High) Illustrative Mathematics does
  the same: Grade 2 Unit 1 introduces tape diagrams **from bar graphs** for compare problems
  **within 20**, then moves to 100 and to unknowns in all positions in Unit 2. (C26 — Confirmed ·
  High) Math in Focus starts bars in Ch 4 at 3-digit size (INVENTORY #9–10).
- **F26. Match the diagram to the story without solving.** An Illustrative Mathematics card sort
  has children match diagrams to stories and **not** solve them; another has them label a tape
  diagram and mark the unknown with "?". (C26 search excerpts — Inferred · Med)
- **F27. Two-step problems: one combined bar or one bar per step — both accepted.** (C19 —
  Confirmed · Med) A recap rule on the same page: the longer bar shows the greater value.
- **F28. Singapore centres still teach "highlight key words", then use the labelled model to
  catch misleading ones.** The Learning Lab tells P1–P2 children to highlight key phrases, label
  every part (who has more, who has fewer), and substitute the answer back; it says "more than"
  can mislead and the model shows when to subtract. It also says P2 children are expected to draw
  their own models. (C18 — Confirmed · Med; the P2 drawing claim is the centre's, not the
  ministry's → Inferred · Med) This sits beside, not against, R2's IES "avoid key words": the
  practice in Singapore centres is key words **checked by** the model.
- **F29. Model order in a vendor guide:** objects → plain blocks → bars without unit marks →
  later fractions, ratio, decimals; two foundational ideas named (part-whole, change). (C17 —
  Confirmed · Med; credibility Low)
- **F30. "No marks for the model itself" at P2 in at least one Singapore school.** A 2025 P2
  deck (now 404) told parents marks are not given for models drawn; model drawing is a heuristic
  for later. The same deck asked parents to secure bonds within 10 and the tables and to set a
  time limit on practice papers. (C21 Kheng Cheng, search excerpt — Inferred · Low) Consistent with R4's ministry rule
  that any sound working earns marks.

### 2.6 Word-problem reading, problem-solving routines, heuristics

- **F31. A five-step routine for P1–P2, four-step (Pólya) from P3.** A ministry school tells
  parents to use "a 5-step approach to problem solving (Primary 1 and 2)"; the step names sit in
  an image we could not read. (C20 — Confirmed · Med; steps absent) A second school's tips repeat
  "4-step for P3–P6, 5-step for P1 and P2". (search excerpt — Inferred · Low)
- **F32. Explain in your own words, then check.** Centre advice across C18 and the parents' pages
  converges on: read slowly (aloud at P1–P2), restate the story, say what is asked, label the
  model, substitute the answer back. (C18 Confirmed · Med; others excluded as SEO farms)

### 2.7 Practice per sitting, review and preview

- **F33. Oak's lesson anatomy:** a **6-question starter quiz** on prior knowledge, two to three
  "learning cycles" (explain → check for understanding → practice with feedback), a **6-question
  exit quiz** on the key points; a printable worksheet carries the practice. (C12 — Confirmed ·
  High across five lessons read)
- **F34. Dimensions' differentiation advice:** for children who struggle — placement test, more
  guidance and manipulatives, **fewer assigned problems**, use the basic workbook part as
  reteaching, use tests for review not grading; for advanced children — less guidance, invent
  their own methods, challenge problems, the harder test. (C7 — Confirmed · High)
- **F35. The Singapore approach is described to parents as spiral** (a P2 handbook, now 404;
  search excerpt — Inferred · Low); Dimensions 2B re-opens mental ± after the 2A written methods
  (C4 — Confirmed · High), i.e. mental routes on 2- and 3-digit numbers come **after** the column.
- **F36. Video lessons are 7–10 minutes** in the online Dimensions programme; the tutor works
  some "Do" problems and the child finishes the rest; the programme warns it suits children who
  like several strategies, less so those who need "lots of repetition". (C9 — Confirmed · Med)

### 2.8 Parent guidance

- **F37. Dimensions' chapter letter is a fixed four-part shape:** what the chapter teaches → the
  class's language with one worked picture → what may look different from how adults learned it
  (e.g. two subtraction methods, one "often more challenging for adults") → **"What can we do at
  home?"** with two to four games and a reminder to use the book's language and ask the child to
  explain. (C6 letters #1–#5 — Confirmed · High) This fills INVENTORY §3 item 10 (no public
  school-to-home letter) for a sister Singapore programme.
- **F38. Fact practice at home is sorted, not timed against a clock:** a three-pile sort of fact
  cards — known at once, known slowly, not known — repeated until all are in the first pile.
  (C6 #2 — Confirmed · High) Our DD13 leaves fluency drill to Kumon-style supply; the sort is a
  parent-side idea only.
- **F39. Singapore schools and vendors steer parents to the ministry's online platform (SLS) and
  KooBits for home practice** (C20 — Confirmed · Med); no school page found gives a daily item
  count.

### 2.9 Free interactive tools

- **F40. Math Learning Center apps.** Number Pieces: base-ten pieces to build, regroup, add,
  subtract; drawing and math-text tools to label. Number Line: whole-number, fraction, decimal or
  blank lines, custom ticks, forward/backward jumps drawn above and below the line. Number
  Frames: 5-, 10-, 20-, 100-frames. Free, but licensed for personal/internal/educational use
  only, with no derivatives and no building a competing or interoperating product. (C23 —
  Confirmed · High) No reviewer evidence on learning effects was found.
- **F41. MOE iwant2study bar-model simulations** (part-part-whole, fractions, word problems,
  percentage) are browser sims by a ministry educator; the licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The page
  does not describe the interaction in enough detail to judge it. (C22 — Confirmed licence · High;
  interaction Inferred · Low)
- **F42. Polypad number bars** support a 1–100 tray game (bars sized by a dice sum placed on a
  grid); Amplify describes bars for + and − and place value. (C24 — Inferred · Med)
- **F43. No free tool found that builds a bar model with a "?" as a decision sequence** other
  than Thinking Blocks (R4). Didax lists place-value disks and base-ten blocks but no bond or bar
  tool (C25 — Inferred · Low). The mathsbot bar-modelling page is gone (404).

## 4. What we found

1. Free Grade 1–2 placement tests from the Singapore Math Inc. family (Dimensions 1A–2B; Primary
   Mathematics 2022 readiness 2A/2B) — this corrects gap G19; two placement models (mastery of the
   last level vs readiness for the next); untimed, unscored, error-watching guidance; ~20–23
   questions per half-grade; difficulty ladders inside one question. (F1–F7)
2. The first public, free Singapore-lineage **school-to-home letters** for Grade 2 (Dimensions),
   with a stable four-part shape and home games. (F37–F38)
3. Two **open-licence** (OGL v3.0) bodies of mastery-lineage teaching text for the US Grade 2 band
   — Oak National Academy (Collection 2) and the DfE Year 3 guidance — with sequences,
   misconceptions and language frames for place value to 1,000, column + and −, and missing
   numbers. (F10–F24, F33; licence table b)
4. An official statement of the Q3 mechanism: with 3-digit numbers, missing-number items need
   rearranging and this does not transfer automatically; three missing-number types (addend,
   subtrahend, minuend) and a part-or-whole sort step. (F22–F23)
5. Two lineages that **introduce bars with numbers within 20** before 3-digit work (Dimensions
   Grade 2 Ch 2; Illustrative Mathematics Grade 2 Unit 1). (F25)
6. Non-standard partitioning, unitising ("how many tens"), number-line positioning to 1,000 as
   parts of the Grade 2-band place-value unit. (F10–F13)
7. "Blocks for structure, facts for answers"; unitising language per column; efficient order for
   three or more addends; column not always best. (F14–F19)
8. A P1–P2 five-step problem-solving routine in Singapore schools (steps unnamed); Singapore
   centres pair key words with a labelled model. (F28, F31)
9. Free manipulative tools and their licences: MLC apps (restrictive), Polypad, Didax, MOE sims
   (CC BY-NC-SA). (F40–F43)
