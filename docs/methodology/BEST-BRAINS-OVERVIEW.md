# Best Brains Method — Overview

*A plain-English overview of the Best Brains teaching approach and how the **Best Brains Method** module in Mind Foundry implements it. Written for parents and product decisions. Two layers are kept distinct throughout: (1) **the real Best Brains program** — what public research established about the franchise, and (2) **our module** — an original, Best Brains-*inspired* build (all questions, explanations, level names, and sequences are 100% original; nothing is copied from Best Brains).*

*Evidence for every factual claim about the real program traces to the frozen research ledger (`modules/best-brains/research/phase2-gaps/EVIDENCE-LEDGER-FINAL.md`, cited as E#). Design choices trace to `DESIGN-DEFAULTS.md` (DD#) and the curriculum map.*

---

## 1. What Best Brains is (the real program)

Best Brains is a U.S. learning-center franchise (founded 2011, ~200+ centers) serving children **ages 3–14** in Math, English, Abacus, and Coding (E-rows S1). Its Math program markets itself as **"non-repetitive"** and **"concept-based"** — explicitly positioned *against* memorization and drill: for ages 6–9 the official page states flatly *"memorization is not the way to learn"* (E106). Lessons are taught by **certified teachers** in small groups (**no more than 5 students per teacher**; online is 1 teacher : ≤3 students), which is the brand's core differentiator versus self-paced worksheet programs (E7, E9).

**The one-line summary:** *one new concept each week, taught by a real teacher, reinforced with short daily homework, graded weekly, and reported home to the parent.*

## 2. The weekly rhythm (the heart of it)

Best Brains runs a weekly cycle it frames as **Teach → Perceive → Practice → Perfect** (E42):

1. **One concept per week.** A single new idea is introduced each week — never a review-heavy grind (E11, E100).
2. **A weekly class** (~30 minutes per subject) where the teacher introduces and explains the concept (E10, E96).
3. **Short daily homework** at home from a weekly packet — one page a day (E99).
4. **Weekly grading**: the packet is graded (by staff against answer keys) and marked **Passed** or **Review** with a percentage; a "Review" triggers **reteach/reassign** before the child moves on (E80, E98).
5. **A weekly parent report** — the signed **Progress Book**, whose four fields are: *what was covered · what needs reinforcement · where the child is improving · what to focus on at home* (E102).

Advancement is **mastery-based, not calendar-based** — a child moves forward when ready, and the curriculum is a **level ladder decoupled from school grade** (a free placement test sets the starting level) (E30, E31).

## 3. How much time per day?

| | Real Best Brains | Our module (Best Brains Method) |
|---|---|---|
| Daily homework | **5–15 minutes** per subject, "about one page," consistency over volume (E12, E99, E106) | Age-banded: **~5–8 min** (ages 4–6), **~10–12 min** (6–9), **~15 min** (10–12); **hard caps 10 / 15 / 20 min** with an adaptive early-stop if the child tires (DD-LS1-R1/R2) |

The doctrine on both sides is identical: **brief, daily, and finishable** beats long marathon sessions. Our caps are grounded in the distributed-practice research (a 10-minute session gains as much as 20–40 minutes for a new skill).

## 4. How many questions per day?

The unit that matters is **one cognitively substantial item** — a word problem, a puzzle, a small computation set with a single strategy — not a page of near-identical sums.

| | Real Best Brains | Our module |
|---|---|---|
| Per day | **~1 page**, low density (~3–6 problems/page; deliberately not a wall of drill) (E55–E60) | **4–6 items** (ages 4–6), **6–8** (6–9), **8–10** (10–12) substantial items, of which **20–30% are spaced review** of earlier weeks (DD8) |
| Weekly variety | word problems, real-world application, timed quizzes, puzzles (E26) | same four levers, plus a dedicated **Day-5 non-computational page** (puzzle / logic / "explain why") every week |

Contrast this with drill-based programs where a day can be 100+ calculations. Here, six varied items are more effortful — and more durable — than sixty repetitions.

## 5. How many modules per level?

| | Real Best Brains | Our module |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | **~26 numbered weeks per level**; ~16 booklets spanning levels (Baby Genius Jr/Sr, then 0a/0b through 8a/8b); Level 7 ≈ Pre-Algebra, Level 8 ≈ Algebra (E93, recovered via archived pages) | **5 levels (A–E)**, each a fixed sequence of **24 weekly concepts** — 2 blocks of 12 with a **Mid-Level Checkpoint (week 12)** and a **Level-Exit Gate (week 24)** (DD2, curriculum map) |
| Grading cadence | weekly grading; retaught before advancing | weekly **85% mastery check**; below that → a one-week **corrective reteach loop** with brand-new (Form B) problems, then re-check (DD1) |
| Level ↔ grade | level ≈ grade, decoupled by placement | A≈PreK/K · B≈Gr 1–2 · C≈Gr 2–3 · D≈Gr 4–5 · E≈Gr 6–7 (typical, never gating) |

So a full journey through our module is **5 levels × 24 weeks = 120 weekly concepts**, PreK-readiness through middle-school-readiness.

## 6. What concepts, by grade/level?

**Our module's ladder** (original sequencing synthesized from the standard CCSS K–7 trajectory; representative concepts):

| Level | Ages · Grade | The big ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | ~4–6 · PreK/K | Count & write to 20; compare/order to 10; **number bonds** to 10; add/subtract within 10; flat & solid shapes; measurable attributes; "find the hidden part" icon puzzles (the first seed of algebra) |
| **B** | ~6–8 · Gr 1–2 | Place value to 120; ±within 20 (strategic) and within 100 (regrouping); the **equal sign as balance** + missing addends; time to the quarter hour; money; picture/bar graphs; arrays as repeated addition; halves & quarters |
| **C** | ~7–9 · Gr 2–3 | ±within 1,000; **all ×/÷ facts** (built by strategy-then-retrieval, never timed drill); **fractions as numbers** with equivalence & comparison; two-step problems; time/mass/volume; area vs. perimeter; classifying quadrilaterals; scaled graphs |
| **D** | ~9–11 · Gr 4–5 | Multi-digit × and ÷; **full fraction arithmetic** (± unlike denominators, ×, ÷ by unit fractions); decimals (place value, compare, arithmetic); expressions & order of operations; Quadrant-I coordinate plane; angles & shape hierarchies; volume; writing short justifications |
| **E** | ~10–12 · Gr 6–7 | Ratios, rates & proportions; **percent** (tax/tip/discount/interest); negative numbers, absolute value & rational-number operations; fraction division; **algebraic expressions + 1- and 2-step equations & inequalities**; four-quadrant plane; circle area/circumference; surface area & volume |

A single thread — *meeting the unknown* — runs the whole ladder: an icon-as-hidden-number in Level A → a balance scale and missing factors in B–C → variables, expressions, and equations in D–E.

**The real Best Brains topic strands, for reference**, published by age band (E10, E55–E60): number sense & counting; addition & subtraction; multiplication & division; decimals & fractions; probability & statistics; algebra & geometry — with a **computational and a non-computational strand every week**. Ages 3–5 focus on number ID/sequence/counting and addition to 10; ages 6–9 add word problems, place value, time, money, graphs, measurement and logic puzzles; ages 10+ move into fractions, decimals, ratios, percents, variables, equations, geometry and functions.

## 7. How our module delivers it

- **Ms. Wren**, a warm, concept-first AI teacher, explains the *why* before the *how*, gives hints before answers (a 3-rung ladder, never a straight answer), and praises strategy over speed.
- **Placement, not grade**: a short, un-test-like activity finds the child's starting level; neutral letter names (A–E) avoid grade-label stigma.
- **The weekly cycle in-app**: a "This Week" hub → lesson → guided practice (worked examples that fade) → short daily practice with immediate, explained feedback → a Day-5 puzzle/reasoning page → a Friday mastery check → a warm resolution ("Passed" or "one more round" — **the child never sees a %, a red mark, or the word "fail"**).
- **The parent side** reproduces the Progress-Book ritual: a weekly narrative in the four official fields, a verdict shown warmly, a "Seen it" acknowledgement (the digital signature), plus trends, a mastery map, error-pattern insights, and "what to say to your child" coaching.

**Three deliberate improvements over the real program** (each flagged in our design as an intentional divergence):
1. **Immediate feedback** on every item (the real program's weekly-batch human grading is a paper-and-staffing artifact, not pedagogy).
2. **Engineered spaced review** woven into daily practice (the real program only revisits material when a child *fails*).
3. **Perfect teacher continuity** — the #1 real-world complaint about Best Brains is teacher turnover breaking the relationship; an AI teacher never churns.

## 8. What's built vs. what's designed (testing note)

The full design is 5 levels × 24 weeks. **Seeded content today** covers **Levels A, B, and C, week 1** (plus a few reference weeks) — enough to test a real placement → lesson → daily practice → weekly check → report cycle. Deeper weeks and Levels D–E are designed and specced but not yet generated. Use a **fresh child profile** to see the clean placement flow; progress persists to the live database.
