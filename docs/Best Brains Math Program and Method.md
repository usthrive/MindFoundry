## Program basics

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Best Brains publicly markets itself as a learning-center franchise serving children roughly ages **3 to 14**. The company’s main and center pages position **Math** as one of the core programs alongside **English, Abacus, and Coding**. Math also appears in related offerings such as **Baby Genius Math** for ages 3–5 and in the **Early Learners** day program, where Baby Genius Math is bundled into a broader half-day early-childhood format. citeturn2view3turn24view0turn23search4turn28view3

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Best Brains currently offers both **in-center** and **online** formats. The homepage advertises “Virtual and In-Person Options,” and the company’s online platform pages describe **bbConnect** as taking the in-center experience to a tablet, with weekly live class, daily homework, and weekly feedback. It also offers **bbSupport** for homework help tied to Math and English enrollment. citeturn2view0turn14view0turn12view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Public footprint signals show a sizable franchise. The franchise site says Best Brains has **over 200 locations in the US, Canada and Australia**. Local center pages also show a strongly after-school operational pattern, often listing weekday evening and Saturday hours. Examples include Edison (Tue/Thu evenings plus Saturday), Livingston (Tue/Thu evenings plus Saturday), and Schaumburg (weekday evening schedule plus Saturday, with one explicitly marked online slot). citeturn4search0turn28view2turn28view1turn28view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Public pricing exists on many center pages, but it varies by franchise. Recent examples show **1 subject per month** at about **$149–$159**, **2 subjects** at about **$259–$269**, and wider variation for **3–4 subjects**. Schaumburg lists $149 / $259 / $299 / $399; Livingston lists $159 / $269 / $299 / $399; Edison lists $159 / $269 / $379 / $479; the online learning center lists $159 / $269 / $299 / $399. The consistent disclaimer is that pricing is **per child per month** and may vary by location. citeturn28view0turn28view1turn28view2turn28view3

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** In the overall portfolio, Math appears to be both a **flagship core subject** and a **feeder spine** for adjacent offerings. Public pages repeatedly place Math in the center of the company’s school-support claims, while Baby Genius Math builds the earliest number-sense pipeline and local “advanced math” pages frame later-stage math enrichment as a mastery path toward pre-algebra, algebra, and STEM readiness. citeturn2view3turn24view0turn27view1turn27view2

## Stated philosophy and differentiators

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Best Brains’ core corporate differentiator is its claim of a **“non-repetitive”** and **concept-based** methodology. On the homepage, the company says its “non-repetitive teaching methodology teaches your child lifelong skills, not memorization” and that its curriculum brings together “logical reasoning and critical thinking” for a “holistic understanding” of concepts. The homepage also says its “concept-based approach” helps learners improve with every lesson. citeturn2view3

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Best Brains explicitly contrasts itself with **memorization-based** learning. The main Math page says concepts are taught “one at a time,” and that Best Brains uses “word problems, real-world applications, timed quizzes and puzzle solving.” The same page states flatly that, for ages 6–9, “Memorization is not the way to learn,” and says the program instead builds foundations through word problems, higher-level critical-thinking problems, and logic puzzles. Some local center pages intensify the contrast by describing their approach as different from “repetitive drills.” citeturn10view0turn0search7

**Confirmed | High confidence.** The teacher claim is central to the brand. Corporate pages repeatedly emphasize **real, certified teachers** rather than self-paced software or assistants. An official Best Brains article says Math and English are taught by **board-certified teachers** and that Math/English groups are capped at **no more than 5 students per teacher** at a table or online session, which the company says guarantees one-on-one interaction in a productive environment. Another official local-center article says, “we don’t just tutor — we teach,” and contrasts Best Brains with programs that rely on “self-paced learning or student assistants.” citeturn2view3turn9view0turn26view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Best Brains also sells the model as **incremental**, not marathon-based. The corporate “Why Us?” page says daily practice at a steady pace yields the “best, most lasting results.” Local official articles repeat this idea, arguing that one weekly class plus short daily homework is more effective than occasional long sessions, and that progress compounds through consistency rather than cram-style repetition. citeturn2view1turn18view1turn18view0

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** The public messaging positions Best Brains closer to **teacher-led academic enrichment** than to a pure tutoring-on-demand or worksheet-drill model. That inference comes from official language about sequential curriculum, concept mastery, small-group direct instruction, daily reinforcement, and the distinction between “teaching” and “just tutoring.” citeturn26view0turn27view2turn27view1

## Reconstructed operating rhythm

**Confirmed | High confidence.** The cycle starts with **placement / assessment / orientation**. The homepage says a child takes a **FREE Placement Test** to determine placement. Center pages commonly advertise a **free consultation and placement test**, and the Baby Genius article adds that the center director assesses the child’s current knowledge to choose the right Junior or Senior Baby Genius placement. citeturn2view0turn28view1turn28view2turn19view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Next comes a **weekly class**. Corporate pages say classes are held **once per week**. In the 2020 official explainer, Best Brains says teachers teach the **concept of the week**, using the weekly homework packet as the learning vehicle. bbConnect’s FAQ is even more concrete: students attend a weekly scheduled live class with up to **3 other students and 1 certified teacher** and receive **30 minutes of live instruction each week per subject**. citeturn9view0turn14view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** The instructional logic inside the week is: **new concept introduction → guided understanding → distributed reinforcement**. Corporate sources say students learn something new every week, concepts are taught “one at a time,” and the weekly packet/booklet is then used to reinforce the concept in multiple ways. Local official pages describe the curriculum as forward-moving and sequential, not review-heavy. citeturn10view0turn9view0turn26view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** The at-home component is **daily homework**, not one bulk weekly assignment. Corporate pages say students practice concepts daily at home; bbConnect says each day the student logs in to complete a section of the same booklet used in the in-person program; and bbSupport is described as 24/7 help with Best Brains Math and English homework. citeturn2view0turn14view0turn12view0

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** Public homework-time guidance clusters around **short daily blocks**, but Best Brains does not publish one single universal number for every format and age. The 2020 corporate explainer recommends **10 minutes per subject per day**; the official online how-to guide says parents “shouldn’t be spending more than **15 minutes per subject** on homework”; and a recent official local article says the program is built around **5–10 minutes per day** or about **one page** completed consistently. Taken together, the public message is that homework is meant to be **brief, daily, and manageable**, not lengthy. citeturn9view0turn2view5turn18view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Homework is then **graded weekly**, and feedback is built into the cycle. bbConnect says homework is submitted weekly for grading, that feedback is provided on performance and progress, and that at the end of each class parents receive feedback on progress, behavior, and areas for improvement. It also says homework may be **reassigned and retaught** so the student can perfect each concept before moving on. Corporate pages generally summarize this as weekly personalized feedback as the child advances through the curriculum. citeturn14view0turn2view0turn2view1

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** Advancement appears to be **mastery-based**, not strictly calendar-based. The main Math page says progress is monitored to keep students moving forward through “our levels.” bbConnect says students may be retaught or reassigned until they perfect the concept before moving to a new topic. Official local pages further describe the curriculum as **level-based** and say students advance when they are genuinely ready, not simply when time has passed. citeturn10view0turn14view0turn27view0turn27view1

**Confirmed and Inferred | Mixed confidence.** **How long is a class?** For **bbConnect / online Math**, the answer is confirmed: **30 minutes of live instruction per subject each week**. For the youngest in-person Baby Genius students, an official article also says **30 minutes a week**. For the broader in-center Math program, public evidence strongly suggests a similar short-slot model, but Best Brains does not publish one universal corporate in-center duration on the main Math page. That inference is supported by the official online guide saying Math and English classes are “a half hour each,” by reviews that call the class “exactly 30 minutes,” and by center schedules built around many short after-school slots. citeturn14view0turn19view0turn6search4turn15search8

## Curriculum signals

**Confirmed | High confidence.** The main corporate Math page gives the clearest corporate-wide topic map: **number sense and counting; addition and subtraction; multiplication and division; decimals and fractions; probability and statistics; algebra and geometry**. It also explicitly says math instruction mixes **computational and non-computational** concepts each week. citeturn10view0turn14view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** For ages **3–5**, public sources show an early-number-sense curriculum centered on **number identification, number sequence, writing numbers, manipulatives**, and foundational number fluency. The Baby Genius Math page adds **number comprehension, counting manipulatives, number sequence/order, number recognition, and addition 0–10**. The Baby Genius explainer says those early levels prepare students for **subtraction and two-digit addition**. citeturn10view0turn24view0turn19view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** For ages **6–9**, the public math-page sample text explicitly emphasizes **word problems**, **higher-level critical thinking problems**, and **logic puzzles** as the anti-memorization core. Official local grade-level pages on the Best Brains domain make that range look like a progression from stronger number sense and single-digit operations into **addition/subtraction within 20, place value, time, money, graphs, measurement, shapes, and two-step word problems**, with deeper work on understanding rather than only answer production. citeturn10view0turn21view1turn21view2

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** For ages **10+**, the official corporate page is broad rather than specific, saying only that older students keep building tools they need to excel. But official local Best Brains grade-level guides strongly suggest that the upper range moves through **fractions, decimals, multi-step problem solving, ratios, percents, variables, equations, geometry, statistics, functions, and linear equations**, depending on grade. That is a reasonable public-source reconstruction of the 10+ band, but it is still partly inferred because the central corporate Math page does not publish a unified 10+ scope-and-sequence. citeturn10view0turn22view3turn22view0turn22view1turn22view2

**Confirmed | High confidence.** There is clear public evidence for **“one concept at a time”**, **level progression**, **word problems**, **logic puzzles**, and **timed work**. The main Math page uses all of that language directly. Local official pages extend the progression claim by describing the curriculum as **level-based**, sequential, and mastery-driven. citeturn10view0turn27view0turn27view2

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** There is some public evidence for **mental math**, but it is not as central on the corporate Math page as the other features. Official local pages mention **mental math strategies** in grade-level math descriptions and say Best Brains’ Math and Abacus programs are designed to boost **mental math skills**. Cary’s advanced-math page also presents Abacus as part of the broader Best Brains approach to number sense, concentration, and timed-assessment performance. Because the strongest “mental math” language is often tied to **Math + Abacus together**, this should be read as a real signal, but not as a pure stand-alone Math-page pillar. citeturn21view1turn22view3turn26view0turn27view2

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Public level names are only partially visible. Corporate sample-page captions expose broad bands such as **Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced** for Math sample pages, and the Baby Genius article says early learners may be placed into **Junior or Senior Baby Genius**. I did **not** find a complete public corporate list of Math levels, level numbers, or exact promotion thresholds. citeturn10view0turn19view0

## Teacher and parent roles

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Best Brains’ public teacher standard is certification-first. Corporate sources say Math and English are taught by **board-certified teachers**; bbConnect says it uses **state or Canadian provincial board-certified teachers**; Baby Genius teachers are described as having degrees in early learning or being state certified for Early Childhood or K–5; and local centers often market “certified and experienced teachers.” citeturn9view0turn14view0turn19view0turn28view1

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Public sources describe the teacher’s class role as **direct instruction**, not passive monitoring. Teachers introduce the concept of the week, provide one-on-one interaction inside small groups, tailor lessons in real time, complete progress updates, and in bbConnect give parent feedback immediately after class about progress, behavior, and areas for improvement. citeturn9view0turn26view0turn14view0turn18view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** Public job postings reinforce that the classroom-teacher role is tightly scoped. Recent Indeed postings for Best Brains say the job is for **certified teachers** working with ages **3–14** in Math and English, that **all teaching materials are provided**, and that teachers have **no lesson plans to write**. Those same postings also say teachers have **no homework to grade**. citeturn8search2turn16search4

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** The public record on **who grades homework** is mixed. Official Best Brains pages clearly say homework is graded weekly, and one official local article says **teachers grade and review students’ work every week**. But public teacher job postings say teachers do **not** grade homework, while public employee-posted Q&A and reviews describe a **grader** role that grades homework with a key and returns it for correction. The most plausible public-source reconstruction is that Best Brains uses a **teacher-led instructional model** but that some centers also use **separate grading staff or workflows**. Corporate-wide division of labor is not standardized publicly. citeturn14view0turn26view0turn8search2turn16search1turn7search22

**Confirmed | High confidence.** The parent role is unusually explicit in Best Brains’ own writing. Parents are asked to bring the child to class or make sure the child is ready online, provide time for daily practice, and **read and sign the Progress Book** so the center knows the family is following along. Recent official local guidance also tells parents to review the Progress Book after each class and, ideally, share the child’s school syllabus or upcoming units so Best Brains can reinforce overlapping concepts. citeturn9view0turn18view0

**Confirmed | High confidence.** The **Progress Book** is the clearest public parent-facing feedback artifact. The 2020 official explainer says parents receive weekly feedback in the child’s Progress Book and are asked to read and sign it. A recent official local article says after every session teachers complete a progress update showing what was covered, what needs reinforcement, where the child is improving, and what to focus on at home. Public review snippets on Best Brains center pages also mention teachers leaving weekly reviews in the Progress Book, which corroborates that the tool is actively used at least in some centers. citeturn9view0turn18view0turn3search2turn3search6

**Inferred | Medium confidence.** Best Brains’ parent-feedback cadence appears to be **at least weekly**, sometimes with a per-session feel. The official minimum is weekly feedback. In bbConnect, parents also get **end-of-class feedback**. That implies a feedback loop that is partly written (Progress Book / reports) and partly verbal or live. What is not publicly clear is whether every center uses the same format or software. citeturn14view0turn9view0turn26view0

## Unknowns

Everything below remains **not determinable from public sources I found**:

- The full corporate-wide Math **level map** beyond broad public bands such as Baby Genius, beginner/intermediate/advanced sample labels, and generic references to “levels.”
- A single universal corporate rule for **in-center Math class length** across all ages and centers.
- A corporate-wide standard for **how many homework pages** or exact tasks are assigned per day or per week for each Math level.
- The exact corporate workflow for **who grades homework** at every center, how quickly it must be returned, and whether grading is teacher-, director-, or grader-owned.
- The exact **placement-test content**, scoring thresholds, and promotion criteria used to move a child from one Math level to the next.
- Whether the **Progress Book** is always physical, always digital, or varies by center / platform.
- A complete, current public list of **all live countries and centers** beyond the franchise site’s over-200-locations claim.
- Whether all public pricing pages include all mandatory fees, or whether some centers add **registration/material fees** that are only disclosed during enrollment.
- Any nonpublic teacher-training protocols, answer keys, internal rubrics, franchise-only manuals, or proprietary worksheet sets. If such materials exist, they were outside the scope of this review and were not used.
